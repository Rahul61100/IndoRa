# Moody's has downgraded the US, and the term premium is up 108bp this year

Verified 2026-08-26. Context I was missing behind the "30-year at 5.2%" observation.

- **Moody's has downgraded the US from Aaa to Aa1**, citing a federal deficit it expects to widen
  toward **~9% of GDP** from 6.4%
- **Term premium up roughly 108bp year to date**
- **10-year hit a 20-month high of 4.75% on 21 August 2026**, easing to ~4.65% — attributed to
  deficit concerns, inflation, and heavy corporate issuance competing with Treasury supply
- **Interest payments on the debt are running 10.6% above the prior year** through ten months of
  FY26
- Aggregate high-yield OAS is only **~275bp** — tight. The stress is specifically in the
  bottom tier ([[private-credit-is-the-real-us-risk]]), not the index.

**Why it matters:** this is the mechanism through which almost every other risk actually bites
equity multiples. A rising term premium is a **structural, always-on drag** that needs no trigger,
and it compounds:
- it is the discount rate under long-duration equities
- it is why a cash-burning AI-capex issuer is now a credit question
  ([[ai-capex-is-now-a-credit-story]])
- it is what any perceived Fed-independence scare would amplify instantly, since the market is
  already primed on exactly this question

**Spillover to India** is direct and compounding: global yield spillover raises EM borrowing costs
and pressures the rupee **at the same time** India is absorbing the oil shock
([[hormuz-is-a-live-war-not-a-risk-premium]], [[CORRECTION-india-did-not-halt-russian-crude]]).
Three pressures on the rupee at once, and the rupee is already the reason dollar returns in India
are negative ([[india-in-usd-is-the-return-that-matters]]).

**Also now on the record:** **Kevin Warsh became the 17th Fed Chair on 22 May 2026** and has
repeatedly asserted independence, framing his mandate around price stability against pressure for
cuts. And the **3 November midterms** show Democrats leading the generic ballot **47.8% to 41.5%**,
with the House map favouring Democratic gains and the Senate more competitive.

**How to apply:** track the **30-year and the term premium**, not the 10-year, as the fiscal
gauge. A divided-government outcome would likely *reduce* incremental fiscal-risk pricing at the
long end while raising near-term policy uncertainty.
