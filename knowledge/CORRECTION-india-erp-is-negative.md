# CORRECTION: India's equity risk premium is NEGATIVE — the G-sec beats the earnings yield

> **AMENDED 2026-08-27 — this note overstates the case.** The −1.96% figure below is a **pre-tax**
> comparison. G-sec interest is taxed at slab with the full surcharge ladder; equity LTCG is 12.5%
> with surcharge capped at 15%. After tax the premium is between **−0.53% and +0.28%** depending on
> income band — roughly zero, not −2%. See [[india-erp-after-tax-is-roughly-zero]]. The direction
> of this note stands; **the magnitude does not.**
>
> **FURTHER AMENDED, same day — the metric itself is near-worthless.** The Fed model has an R² of
> **0.5%** for forward 10-year returns against 56.3% for plain P/E, adding the bond term *reduces*
> predictive power, and Damodaran's rigorous ERP series shows **no negative years at all** since
> 1960. The negative sign is a construction artifact. **This note should carry almost no weight.**
> See [[the-fed-model-has-no-predictive-power]].

**This is the most important correction in the workspace and it undermines the central claim I
made on 2026-08-25.** I said Indian large caps were cheap at 20.4x, "12.6% below their 10-year
average". That is a statement about India versus **its own past**. It is not a statement about
India versus **the return available on cash today**, and on that test the answer inverts.

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| India 10-year G-sec | **6.85%** (up from 6.56% a year ago) |
| India 30-year G-sec | 7.41% |
| Nifty 50 trailing P/E | 20.46x → earnings yield **4.89%** |
| Forward P/E proxies | MSCI India 20.32x; brokerage range 20.5-22x; Nomura 21x |
| **Equity risk premium** | **−1.93% to −2.09%** |

**The ERP is negative by roughly 190-210bp under every input, and that result is robust to the
choice of proxy.** An investor buying the Nifty today accepts a forward earnings yield *below*
what a 10-year government bond pays risk-free.

For scale: standard Indian cost-of-equity practice (Damodaran-style, used by valuers) assumes an
ERP around **7.0%**. The gap between what practice assumes and what the market is currently
accepting is roughly **nine percentage points**. Those are different metrics and must not be
averaged — but it is the sharpest way to state how stretched this reading is.

**Corporate credit is not confirming an easing tailwind either.** AAA sits at 7.0-8.3% and AA at
8.0-9.5%, with spreads over G-sec of 50bp+ on top-rated paper and 100-260bp on AA. The "rates will
fall and rescue the multiple" case has to clear a financing-conditions hurdle that has not
loosened. (Note: sources disagree materially on the AAA spread — 10-140bp by issuer type from one,
221bp from another. Unresolved.)

**How to apply:** **stop quoting the P/E against its own history as evidence of cheapness.** The
only valuation statement worth making about India is the rate-adjusted one, and it currently says
the market is expensive, not cheap. Quote the ERP alongside any multiple, always.

**Unavailable, not estimated:** a clean single-source Nifty-50-specific forward P/E, and a
historical average and range for this exact ERP metric. Both are genuine gaps — the direction of
the finding is solid, the historical percentile is not established.
