# The rupee is being held up, not holding up

Two independent lines of evidence reached the same place today — one from price behaviour, one from
the RBI's own published tables. That agreement is what makes this load-bearing.

## What the price said

Since 1 July, against a dollar that **fell 2.1%**:

- EM median currency: **−1.2%** (strengthened, as they should into dollar weakness)
- **India: +0.6%** (weakened anyway)
- **10th of 12 EM currencies.** Only the Philippine peso and the Turkish lira are worse.
- Over six months: EM median +1.3%, India **+3.7%** — 2.4pp worse.

Korea's won, over the same window, was the strongest in the sample at **−10.8%** (i.e. a large
appreciation).

## What the RBI's own tables say

This settles the contested reserves claim, and both prior readings were partly right about
different windows.

**The stock looks fine.** Reserves were **$716,907mn** on 14 Aug 2026 — up $25.8bn over end-March
and $21.8bn year-on-year, near the year's high. Import cover ~11 months for goods (~9 months
including services), and reserves covered **90% of external debt** at end-December 2025. Anyone
looking only at the headline sees strength.

**The flow does not.** RBI's Bulletin Table 4 shows cumulative **net dollar SALES of $53,132mn in
FY2025-26** — the highest ever recorded, and confirmed independently where the financial press
reports the same $53.13bn figure. That is a record year of selling dollars to defend the rupee.

**And the forward book is the part nobody quotes.** Net forward position as of 31 March 2026:

| maturity | net position (US$mn) |
|---|---|
| ≤1 month | −14,460 |
| 1–3 months | −16,148 |
| 3 months–1 year | −19,653 |
| **>1 year** | **−52,803** |
| **total** | **−103,064** |

**Short across every single maturity bucket — no long position anywhere.** The progression matters
more than the level: −84,345 (Mar 2025) → −63,605 (Oct 2025, narrowing) → −77,666 (Feb 2026) →
**−103,064 (Mar 2026)**. That is a **$25.4bn widening in one month.**

The reserve stock also was not monotonic: it fell **$54bn (7.5%)** from the 27 Feb 2026 peak of
$728.5bn to a 3 July trough of $674.2bn, then rebounded $43bn in six weeks. A single-date snapshot
of this series will support almost any argument, which is why the flow tables matter more.

## What it means

**A headline reserve stock near its high, funded by record dollar sales and a forward book that is
net short $103bn and widening, is not a strong currency. It is a managed one.** The stock is the
output of the defence, not evidence that no defence was needed.

This is the mechanism behind the price observation. The rupee failed to strengthen into broad
dollar weakness because the underlying pressure is large enough that a record intervention year
merely held the line. Everything else in this base points the same way — FPI outflows of ₹2.87 lakh
crore YTD, net FDI near a record low of $7.65bn against record *gross* FDI of $95bn (the gap is
repatriation), a July goods trade deficit of $31.98bn at a six-month high, a current account back
in deficit, and a Russian crude discount that has **inverted to a premium** so the import bill no
longer gets its subsidy.

## What this changes

1. **The ~3.5%/yr rupee depreciation assumed in the base-rate work is not conservative.** It is the
   realised rate over a period that included a record defence. Absent that defence it would have
   been worse.
2. **Intervention capacity is finite and now visible.** The relevant question is no longer "are
   reserves adequate" — on the stock they are — but "how much longer can a $103bn short forward
   book be rolled." That is a different question with a different answer.
3. **This is a political-economy fact, not just a macro one.** A managed currency is a policy
   choice with a constituency, and the choice is being made against a live external deficit. Watch
   the forward book, not the headline.

## The gap

**No forward-book figure newer than 31 March 2026 could be obtained.** RBI's own document host
sits behind a bot-detection challenge that blocked direct retrieval, and the Internet Archive has
no clean capture. One secondary, unverified data point suggests RBI "trims short forward dollar
position to $72.5 billion" — if true that is a material improvement, and it is **not confirmed
against a primary table.** The next Weekly Statistical Supplement was due **28 Aug 2026** —
tomorrow. **Check it.**
