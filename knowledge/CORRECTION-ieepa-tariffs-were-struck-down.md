# CORRECTION: the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February 2026

**This materially qualifies the tariff framing I used on 2026-08-25.** I described the
February 2026 India-US deal as cutting tariffs from 50% to 18% and treated that rate as settled.

**What I missed:** on **20 February 2026 the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 (Roberts writing) that IEEPA
does not authorise tariffs at all.** CBP halted collection on **24 February 2026**. The ruling left
open whether refunds are owed.

**What that means:**
- The administration's *primary* tariff instrument was voided, days after the India deal was
  announced on 2 February.
- Policy is forced onto **narrower and slower statutory authorities** — Section 122, 232, 301-type
  routes — each with its own procedural constraints and timelines.
- There is now a **refund-litigation overhang** and genuine uncertainty about which tariffs are
  legally durable at all.

**Why it matters:** it does not end the trade conflict, and the 18% headline may well be
reconstructed under other authorities. But it changes the *character* of the risk from "a settled
rate we can plan around" to "an unsettled legal question with a live refund liability". Any thesis
resting on tariff certainty — in either direction — is resting on less than it appears.

It also reframes the **generic-pharma escalation schedule** (0% now, 100% in Aug 2028, 200% in Aug
2029, [[generic-pharma-tariff-bomb]]). That schedule needs a legal basis too. Whether it survives
the same challenge is now an open question I have not answered.

**How to apply:** re-verify the operative legal authority before quoting any US tariff rate as
fact. Watch the substitute-authority rulemaking and the refund litigation as the live variables.
**Open question:** does the pharma escalation schedule rest on IEEPA or on a separate authority?
Unresolved — do not assert either way until checked.
