# Nobody is cutting AI capex — and they are extending depreciation schedules to absorb it

Verified 2026-08-26. **The historical work flagged depreciation games as the classic 1999-2001
tell. They are happening now.**

## No one is cutting. Every hyperscaler raised guidance in July-August 2026.

| Company | FY2026 | Prior guidance | Q2 2026 capex |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Microsoft** (FY to June) | **$115.9bn actual**, against $64.6bn in FY25 | — | $35.8bn in Q4 FY26 |
| **Alphabet** | **$195-205bn** | raised from $180-190bn | $44.92bn, a record |
| **Amazon** | **~$220bn** | raised from ~$200bn | $54.2bn |
| **Meta** | **$130-145bn** | raised from $125-145bn | $31.08bn |

Aggregate 2026 estimates cluster at **$700-800bn**. **Morgan Stanley raised its 2027 estimate to
$1.4 trillion on 3 August 2026** — 17% above the prior Street consensus of $1.2tn — specifically
*because* of the guidance beats. Goldman models **$5.3 trillion cumulative for FY2025-30** across
the top four.

**Searching for evidence of capex cuts returned the opposite.** The only caution found is
CreditSights flagging capital-efficiency and ROI as an "amber alert" — investor scrutiny, not a
spending pullback.

## The accounting

- **Meta extended server and network useful life to 5.5 years**, cutting FY25 depreciation by
  ~$2.9bn
- **Microsoft extended data-centre and office useful life from 15 to 25 years, effective FY2027**,
  announced on the 29 July 2026 call
- A secondary estimate puts the combined effect at **~$176bn of cumulative understated
  depreciation across 2026-2028, overstating operating income by more than 20%** (not
  company-disclosed — treat as an estimate, not a fact)

**Extending the assumed life of an asset that is obsoleting faster, not slower, is precisely what
telecom did before 2001.** GPUs are not 25-year assets. This does not prove a bubble; it does mean
**reported earnings are flattered and the flattering is discretionary and reversible.**

## Why the concentration risk is systemic, not sectoral

- AI infrastructure investment is roughly **half of 2026 S&P EPS growth** (Goldman)
- AI and IT investment was **92% of US GDP growth in H1 2025** — without it, H1 growth would have
  been ~0.1% (Jason Furman; **no 2026 update found — this figure is stale, flag it when quoting**)

**A single, hyper-concentrated capex cycle is now holding up S&P earnings and US GDP growth at the
same time.** Any hyperscaler deceleration hits both channels together — and it would arrive
alongside a softening real economy: **July payrolls −23,000, a second consecutive weak month, with
May and June revised down a combined −103,000; unemployment 4.1%; Q2 GDP +1.5% annualised, down
from +2.1%.**

Meanwhile **core PCE is +3.3% year on year** (released 26 August). **Softening labour, sticky
inflation, and a Fed with three dissents wanting to hike** ([[fed-may-hike-next]]) is an
uncomfortable combination.

And the consumer is splitting: **auto-loan 90-day delinquency at 5.5% — a series record, above the
2010 GFC peak of 5.3%** — while aggregate household delinquency at 4.7% looks fine. K-shaped.

**The three indicators to watch:** a hyperscaler guiding capex *down*; depreciation assumptions
being *shortened* rather than extended; and the AI-capex share of GDP growth falling. None has
happened. Related: [[ai-capex-is-now-a-credit-story]], [[private-credit-is-the-real-us-risk]].
