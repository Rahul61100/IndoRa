# Q1 FY27 GDP prints 31 August — consensus 7.2-7.3%, and the RBI's own path implies deceleration

Verified 2026-08-27. **Confidence: DEGRADED** — gathered after the session search cap via news
aggregation. **Two internal inconsistencies flagged below; do not quote the annual figure without
checking MoSPI directly.**

## The forecasts

| Institution | Q1 FY27 forecast | Published |
|---|---|---|
| **SBI Research** | **8.0%** | 25 Aug |
| Mint poll | 7.4% | 26 Aug |
| CareEdge | 7.3% | 25 Aug |
| Economic Times poll | 7.3% | 17 Aug |
| **Financial Express poll — median** | **7.2%** | 18 Aug |
| Bank of Baroda | 7.0-7.2% | 21 Aug |
| ICRA | 7.0% | 17 Aug |

Range **7.0% to 8.0%**, clustering at **7.2-7.3%**. The FE poll explicitly frames that as **a
four-quarter low**.

**SBI at 8.0% is 100bp above the cluster** — an unusually wide dispersion for a print four days
away, and a reminder that the new series has made forecasting harder, not easier.

## The thing the print itself will not tell you

**The RBI's own quarterly path implies sharp deceleration after Q1.** Its 5 August projection put
FY27 at **6.7%** (nudged up from 6.6%), with **Q2 6.4%, Q3 6.5%, Q4 6.8%.**

So if Q1 lands at 7.2-7.3%, the RBI is already modelling a fall to the mid-6s for the rest of the
year. **A strong Monday print is therefore not a trend — it is the high-water mark of the RBI's own
forecast path.** Anyone trading a beat as evidence of acceleration is reading one quarter against a
projection that expects the opposite.

## Two unreconciled figures — flag before quoting

**1. FY26 full-year growth: I have been quoting 7.7%. This research says 7.6%** (NSO, 5 June 2026).
Both come from post-revision reporting. **Unresolved — check MoSPI's own release before using
either.** I have used 7.7% repeatedly in earlier notes.

**2. The quarterly figures do not average to the annual.** Reported prints are Q1 FY26 **7.8%**,
Q2 **8.2%**, Q3 **7.8%**, Q4 **7.8%** — which cannot produce a full year of 7.6%, or of 7.7%.
Either the quarterly series has been revised and the reporting is mixing vintages, or one of these
numbers is wrong. **Do not build anything on the quarterly path until this reconciles.**

## Why comparability is genuinely impaired right now

The new series, announced February 2026, **rebased from 2015-16 to 2022-23** and:
- **eliminated the "discrepancies" balancing item** that previously absorbed measurement gaps
- made **GST data the backbone**, improving services and informal-sector capture
- updated the price deflators

**The full historical back-series is not available until February 2027.** Until then, any
cross-year comparison mixes methodologies, which is very likely the source of the two
inconsistencies above.

## How to apply

Monday's number is a **level**, not a direction. The direction is in the RBI's own quarterly path,
and it points down. Watch the **GVA** figure alongside headline GDP — the gap between them is where
net-indirect-tax distortion shows, and GST 2.0 rate cuts make that gap unusually informative this
year. GVA for recent quarters was **not found** in this pass; it is a genuine collection gap.
