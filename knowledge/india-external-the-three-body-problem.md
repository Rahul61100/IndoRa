# India's external position is a three-body problem, and I have been analysing one body

**Reasoning, 2026-08-27.** Not new research — a reframe of facts already in this base that I had
been holding separately and never connected. Agents are verifying the components; the structure
below is mine and is labelled as reasoning.

## The bind

India needs three things from three powers that are in conflict with each other:

- **The United States** — market access for the $18bn-plus goods surplus and for IT services, plus
  capital
- **China** — the physical inputs for every manufacturing ambition India has
- **Russia** — historically ~60% of defence hardware, and until recently discounted crude

**Every move toward one antagonises another, and India has just been forced to move.**

The February 2026 US deal cut tariffs 50% → 18% **and required halting Russian crude purchases.**
I recorded that as a clean win. It was not. The related finding
([[CORRECTION-india-did-not-halt-russian-crude]]) is that India did not really halt them by choice
— **China outbid India for the discounted barrels while Ukrainian strikes cut supply.**

**So India paid a strategic price to the US for something it was simultaneously losing to China on
commercial terms.** The crude import bill is **+56.5% to $63.4bn on flat volumes**. India gave up
the lever and did not get the benefit.

## The manufacturing contradiction I have been ignoring

I have written repeatedly about the **₹1.9 lakh crore** electronics and semiconductor push —
Semicon 2.0 and Mobile PLI 2.0 — as a clean policy tailwind, and about Dixon as its expression.

**I never once asked where the components come from.**

India cannot manufacture electronics at scale without Chinese components, sub-assemblies,
rare-earth magnets, solar cells, capital equipment and technical personnel — **while restricting
Chinese capital under Press Note 3 and holding an unresolved border.**

**The tell I already had and did not read:** the **Dixon-Vivo JV was approved under Press Note 3 in
July 2026.** A government that restricts Chinese FDI approving a Chinese JV in its flagship
manufacturing sector is not a contradiction to be explained away — **it is the policy admitting the
dependency.**

And the timing sharpens it: **China's rare-earth export-control suspension expires 10 November
2026** ([[china-rare-earth-deadline-is-november-10]]) — one week after the US midterms. I logged
that as a US risk. **It is at least as much an India risk**, and I filed it in the wrong place.

## The current account has three legs and I have looked at one

| Leg | Rough scale | What I did |
|---|---|---|
| **Goods trade** — dominated by the oil bill | crude alone $63.4bn Apr-Jul | analysed, at length |
| **Services exports** — IT | ~$200bn+ | analysed **only as an AI-disruption story**, never as a trade-policy one |
| **Remittances** | India is **the world's largest recipient** | **never mentioned once** |

**Remittances are the omission that bothers me most.** Roughly **9 million Indians work in the
Gulf.** A large share of the inflow originates there.

**So the Hormuz conflict is not only an oil-price event.** It is a **remittance and household-income
event** concentrated in Kerala, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and Tamil Nadu — and it puts nine million
Indian citizens in the neighbourhood of a shooting war with two failed ceasefires
([[hormuz-is-a-live-war-not-a-risk-premium]]). I have been pricing the barrel and ignoring the
people.

**The tail nobody prices:** services exports and remittances together offset most of the goods
deficit. **AI disrupting IT services and a Gulf disruption hitting remittances would remove both
offsets simultaneously** — and those two risks are usually modelled as unrelated. They are not
unrelated to the current account; they are additive to it.

That, in turn, is what the RBI is defending in a **95.4-95.7 band** with intervention rather than
equilibrium ([[session-2026-08-27-india]]). **Three pressures on the rupee at once, and I have been
treating the band as stability.**

## Two positions I held on the wrong side of this

**HAL.** ~60% of India's defence hardware is historically Russian, with live **CAATSA** exposure,
while the US deal requires alignment. **I owned HAL and never once considered that its supply chain
and its customer's geopolitical alignment are in tension.** I closed it on a governance chain
([[governance-screen-aug-2026]]) — the right decision reached without seeing the larger risk.

**Infosys and TCS.** I framed Indian IT purely as an **AI-disruption** story. It is also a
**trade-policy** story — H-1B, data localisation, digital services taxes, EU data adequacy — and I
have no coverage of any of it. **A technology thesis on a sector whose access to its market is a
negotiated variable is half a thesis.**

## What this changes about method

**I have been analysing India as a closed economy with a tariff number bolted on.** The external
sector is not a footnote to the domestic story — for a country running a structural goods deficit
financed by services and remittances, **it is the constraint that binds everything else**,
including the rupee, which is the variable that turns every INR return into the USD return the
marginal foreign buyer actually sees ([[india-in-usd-is-the-return-that-matters]]).

**Three agents are verifying: what is actually in the US deal, the China dependency in numbers, and
the full alliance and exposure map.** But the structural point does not depend on their findings —
**it depends on my having held these facts separately for three days without connecting them.**
