# Hormuz is an active war with two failed ceasefires, not a risk premium

Verified 2026-08-26. **I had been treating this as a supply disruption. It is a shooting war**,
and the market structure shows where the real stress sits.

**Timeline of 2026:**
- **28 Feb** — Operation Epic Fury kills Khamenei
- **4 Mar** — Iran declares the Strait of Hormuz closed. **Brent goes from ~$60 to $126/bbl —
  the largest monthly move in oil-market history**
- **8 Apr** — ceasefire, which collapses
- **13 Apr** — US Navy blockade of Iranian ports
- **4 May** — Operation Project Freedom begins escorting merchant convoys
- **17 Jun** — Trump-Pezeshkian MOU to end the war
- **8 Jul** — that MOU collapses after Iran strikes multiple commercial ships
- **Now** — the strait is **contested, not closed**. 65+ vessels hit, ~20 seafarers killed since
  February. The US Navy has cleared mines and escorts convoys.

**The market structure is the tell, and it corrects my earlier read.** I noted Brent at $86 and
27% below its high and read that as the spike deflating. But the curve is in **steep
backwardation** — near-dated well above deferred — and **diesel 3-2-1 crack spreads have blown
past $100/bbl against a normal $15-25 range.**

**That says the stress is in the physical market, not the paper market.** A backwardated curve
with blown-out cracks is not a market that has relaxed; it is a market with no spare physical
barrels that expects the problem to resolve later. My "oil is deflating" framing read the flat
price and ignored the curve.

**It also means the deferred contracts are NOT pricing a re-closure** — which is what makes
long-dated upside protection comparatively cheap despite elevated front-end volatility.

**Probability, as assessed:** medium-high that the intermittent-attack equilibrium persists to
year-end (two ceasefires have already failed in five months); low for a full sustained re-closure
given the active de-mining and escort posture; low-medium for a durable verified ceasefire before
the 3 November US midterms, given the electoral incentive to show control without fully
de-escalating.

**Most exposed: India** — roughly **40% of its crude, 60% of its LNG and 90% of its LPG** transit
or originate near Hormuz. Compounded by the loss of the Russian discount
([[CORRECTION-india-did-not-halt-russian-crude]]).

**How to apply:** read the **curve shape and crack spreads**, never the flat price alone. Add both
to the daily macro pull. The US exposure is political (gasoline prices into midterms), not
external — it is a net energy exporter.
