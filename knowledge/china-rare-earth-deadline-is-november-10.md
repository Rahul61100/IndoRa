# China's rare-earth suspension expires 10 November 2026 — a week after the US midterms

Verified 2026-08-26. A dated, calendar-fixed escalation point I did not have.

- China widened export controls to **10 US firms on 22 June 2026** and **14 EU firms on 24 July**
- The **NdPr magnet-alloy benchmark rose 21.4% in the single month to 1 July**
- The IEA has warned full enforcement could put **$6.5 trillion of downstream global production**
  at risk
- Critically: China's original October 2025 controls were **suspended for one year, until
  10 November 2026** — so the current escalation is happening *ahead of* that expiry, and the
  decision point lands **one week after the 3 November US midterms**

**Why it matters:** most geopolitical risk has no date. This one does, and it sits immediately
after an election that will have changed the US political configuration. Transmission runs through
magnet and critical-mineral input costs into **autos, defence, electronics and compound
semiconductors** (GaAs/GaN) — margin pressure and inflation pass-through, not a demand shock.

**Alongside it, Taiwan.** TSMC still holds **~90% of global advanced-node capacity**, essentially
all on Taiwan and within PLA missile range. Arizona's Gigafab Cluster does not reach volume
production until **2H 2027 at the earliest**, so there is **no near-term geographic hedge** for the
AI and compute complex. The US NSC's own estimate of a Chinese move against Taiwan is ~$1 trillion
of global economic disruption. Japan's August 2026 defence white paper judges the military balance
to be "rapidly tilting in China's favour". July saw two-day Chinese live-fire drills; August saw
Taiwan's largest-ever reservist call-up.

**How to apply:** put **10 November** in the calendar as a hard date. It is the kind of event that
is knowable in advance and therefore hedgeable in advance — unlike the yen unwind
([[the-yen-carry-trade-has-reloaded]]), which is not. Most exposed is the US, through index weight
in the semis and AI complex; India is a secondary importer, not a primary battleground.
