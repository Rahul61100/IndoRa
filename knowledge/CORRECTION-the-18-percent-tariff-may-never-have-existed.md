# CORRECTION: the 18% tariff is stale, is probably 10% today, and may never have been collected

Verified 2026-08-27. **I have quoted "the India-US deal cut tariffs from 50% to 18%" in almost
every India note for four days. It is wrong in three separate ways.**

## The rate has moved through four legal authorities in six months

| Period | Rate | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| pre-Feb 2026 | ~50% | 25% reciprocal + 25% Russia-oil penalty |
| **2-20 Feb 2026** | **18% announced** | **IEEPA** |
| **20 Feb** | — | **Supreme Court strikes IEEPA down, 6-3 — thirteen days after the announcement** |
| 20-22 Feb | legal void | CBP halts collection 24 Feb |
| ~24 May - 25 Jul | **10%** | **Section 122** (balance-of-payments, statutorily capped at 150 days) |
| **25 Jul 2026 onward** | **~10%** | **Section 301** |

**"No evidence was found that 18% was ever the number actually collected at customs."** The most
recent reporting puts the real rate at **10%, on a different statute**.

**Gems and jewellery specifically sit at 10% under Section 301** — *below* the headline I have
been quoting. In that case the truth is better than my number, which is not a defence.

## The deal is not in force, and has no published text

- **India's own Commerce Minister said on 29 July 2026** that the first tranche "awaits comparative
  tariff advantage" — **not operational.**
- **Hinrich Foundation called the deal "stalled" on 25 August 2026 — two days ago.**
- **No full legal text has been published**, six months after the announcement.
- **Neither country has completed a ratification process**, because there is no single ratified
  instrument to ratify. India's Parliamentary Standing Committee issued 14 recommendations in
  August — oversight, not ratification.

**What I have been describing as a signed trade deal is an announced political framework with no
text, no ratification and no operative date.** That is exactly the *announced versus delivered*
distinction I wrote a whole note about ([[announced-is-not-disbursed]]) and then failed to apply to
the biggest external fact in my analysis.

## The two parties describe the deal differently

The US framing is that Modi agreed to stop buying Russian oil. **Russia's government stated
"except Trump, no one said India will stop buying Russian oil."** India's Commerce Ministry says
oil sourcing is decided by buyers and the deal does not determine it.

That vindicates the earlier correction ([[CORRECTION-india-did-not-halt-russian-crude]]) — but it
is worse than I framed it. **There may have been no commitment at all.** And the White House
briefly included India's "Google tax" in an official factsheet in February and then quietly deleted
it — the paperwork was sloppy at the moment of announcement.

**A deal whose two parties cannot agree on what was agreed is not durable by construction.**

## Two live threats that post-date everything I recorded

**1. The Senate passed a Russia-sanctions bill 86-11 on 8 August 2026** authorising tariffs of **up
to 100% on India** for Russian crude purchases. Stalled in the House as of mid-August. **If signed,
it overrides the entire February framework.**

**2. A White House "Great Transshipment Scam" report in mid-August placed India in TIER 1** of a
~40-country China-transshipment risk list, alleging **$19-26bn a year** of lost US tariff revenue
and naming **Pune, Gujarat and Chennai** specifically.

**The timing lines up tightly with the "stalled" verdict two days ago.** And note what it means:
the US is alleging India is a conduit for Chinese goods **at the same time** India's manufacturing
base is structurally dependent on Chinese inputs at 18-20% domestic value-add
([[india-value-add-is-stuck-at-18-percent]]). **Those two facts are the same fact viewed from two
capitals.**

## What India actually conceded — narrower than the headlines, but not nothing

**Held:** wheat, rice, dairy, poultry, milk, GM crops.
**Conceded:** dried distillers' grains, sorghum, tree nuts, certain fruits, soybean oil, wine and
spirits, plus non-tariff-barrier and standards alignment.
**Unresolved and still being negotiated as of 20 August:** digital trade, data rules, non-tariff
barriers, rules of origin, financial services, government procurement, multi-brand retail FDI.
**Genuine research gap:** pharmaceutical IP — Section 3(d) and compulsory licensing — and medical
device price caps. **Unknown, not "protected."**

**What the US got that is not tariffs:** ~10% of India's LPG imports sourced from the US — real but
modest. **No crude commitment.** The **$500bn purchase figure is explicitly non-binding** —
Goyal: "We don't have to."

## Two corrections to things I assumed

**Indian pharma is NOT rushing to build US plants.** I assumed the 2028/2029 escalation would force
onshoring. The reported industry position is the opposite — *"Manufacturing in US impossible:
Pharma exporters."* **No named company commitment, no dollar figure.** The dominant sentiment is
that US onshoring is not economically viable at all. **That makes the escalation cliff worse, not
better** — there is no adaptation underway.

**And the pharma schedule is a separate action.** Announced 21-22 July 2026, distinct from and
later than the February framework's zero-duty provision. **Which authority it rests on could not be
confirmed** — plausibly Section 232, which would survive the IEEPA ruling, but that is inference.

**GE Aerospace and HAL finalised the F414 engine technology transfer on 17 April 2026** — relevant
to the HAL supply-chain question I raised and closed the position without answering.

## The method failure

**I took a headline number from a political announcement and used it as a fact for four days
without once asking what authority it rested on or whether it was being collected.** The Supreme
Court voided that authority thirteen days after the announcement — a fact I had *already recorded
separately* ([[CORRECTION-ieepa-tariffs-were-struck-down]]) **and never connected to the tariff
rate I kept quoting.**

**Rule: for any policy number, establish the legal instrument, whether it is in force, and whether
it is actually being applied — before using it even once.**
