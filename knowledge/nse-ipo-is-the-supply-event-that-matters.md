# The NSE IPO is India's largest ever and it prices in September — bigger than Jio

Verified 2026-08-26. I had been tracking the Jio listing as the big supply event. **NSE's is
larger and closer.**

- **Issue size ~₹31,500 crore**, **OFS only** (roughly a 6% stake — no fresh capital, pure supply)
- Price band **₹2,100-2,300**, valuing NSE at **₹5.2-5.3 lakh crore (~$55bn)**
- **India's largest-ever IPO**, targeted to list in **the second half of September 2026**
- SEBI issued its NOC after accepting a **₹1,491 crore co-location settlement**; DRHP filed
  17 June 2026, final observations expected shortly after 19 August

**Why this matters more than its headline:** it is **entirely an offer for sale.** Every rupee
drains the same domestic pool that the SIP bid fills, and none of it goes into a business. Set
against the bid: SIP inflows were **₹31,961 crore in July 2026, a four-month high** — so a single
IPO is roughly **one full month of the entire country's SIP flow.**

Together with Jio Platforms (~$4bn, filed 19 June, window Aug-Oct) and the broader **₹4.72 trillion
pipeline across 238 companies** ([[india-supply-versus-the-sip-bid]]), September and October are
the heaviest supply months India has faced.

**A second-order effect worth holding:** NSE listing puts a **more diversified direct competitor**
onto the board against BSE, which trades at ~47.8x. BSE and MCX already sold off up to 4% on
NSE-IPO news.

**Related regulatory fact:** SEBI's F&O curbs cut retail derivatives participation by roughly
**20%** and NSE derivatives turnover by ~5% (₹213 lakh crore in FY25 to ₹202 lakh crore in FY26).
That is the earnings base NSE is being floated on.

**How to apply:** put **September 2026** in the calendar as an index-level liquidity event, not a
stock event. When ₹31,500 crore of pure secondary supply prices into a market whose foreign
ownership is already at a 14-year low of 14.7%, the marginal domestic rupee has to absorb it, and
that rupee is currently the only bid.
