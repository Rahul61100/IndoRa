# India lost 11% in USD while Korea doubled — and chasing that now is the classic late-cycle error

## The opportunity cost, in the only currency that settles the argument

One-year total returns converted to USD, so a dollar- or rupee-based allocator sees what was
actually earned:

| market | 1y USD | 1y local | FX contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Korea KOSPI** | **+109.3%** | +108.5% | +0.8pp |
| Taiwan TAIEX | +74.7% | +82.5% | −7.8pp |
| Japan Nikkei | +39.5% | +50.9% | −11.4pp |
| Brazil Bovespa | +29.2% | +22.6% | +6.6pp |
| Mexico IPC | +20.1% | +9.4% | +10.7pp |
| Euro Stoxx 50 | +19.8% | +20.4% | −0.6pp |
| S&P 500 | +17.5% | +17.5% | 0.0pp |
| China SSE | +10.2% | +3.8% | +6.4pp |
| **India Nifty** | **−10.9%** | −3.5% | **−7.4pp** |
| Indonesia JCI | −21.3% | −15.3% | −6.0pp |

India is **9th of 10**. It lost money twice: −3.5% in the index and another −7.4pp in the currency.

## The rupee is not just weak, it is weak *relative to its peers*

The level tells you little; the peer comparison tells you a lot. Since 1 July, against a dollar
that **fell 2.1%**:

- EM median: **−1.2%** (peer currencies *strengthened* against a weakening dollar, as they should)
- India: **+0.6%** (the rupee *weakened* anyway)
- India ranks **10th of 12** EM currencies. Only the Philippine peso and the Turkish lira are worse.
- Over six months the gap widens: EM median +1.3%, India +3.7%, India **2.4pp worse**.

A currency that fails to strengthen when the dollar falls and every peer rallies is being told
something idiosyncratic. The candidates are already in this knowledge base and all point the same
way: FPI outflows of ₹2.87 lakh crore YTD, net FDI near a record low of $7.65bn against record
gross FDI of $95bn, a July goods trade deficit of $31.98bn at a six-month high, and a current
account back in deficit. **The rupee's relative underperformance is the market pricing the balance
of payments**, and it is a cleaner read on it than any single monthly print.

Korea's won, by contrast, is the strongest EM currency in the sample — **+10.8%** since 1 July.

## Korea is real. That is exactly why it is dangerous now.

Verified rather than assumed, because a +109% index year deserves it. SK Hynix's quarterly operating
margin, straight from the income statement:

| quarter | revenue (KRW bn) | operating income | margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-03 | 17,639 | 7,441 | 42.2% |
| 2025-06 | 22,232 | 9,213 | 41.4% |
| 2025-09 | 24,449 | 11,383 | 46.6% |
| 2025-12 | 32,827 | 19,170 | 58.4% |
| 2026-03 | 52,576 | 37,610 | **71.5%** |

Revenue tripled in five quarters, operating income grew fivefold, and the trailing operating margin
now prints **76.3%**. The figure flagged as "either a spectacular anomaly or a data error" is
**neither** — it is a real memory supercycle.

**And SK Hynix trades at a forward P/E of 3.7.**

That combination is not a valuation opportunity. **Peak earnings at a trough multiple is the
signature of a cyclical top**, not of something cheap. A 3.7× forward multiple on 76% operating
margins is the market stating plainly that it does not believe those margins persist — and in
memory, the most violently cyclical business in technology, the market is usually right about that.

The tape agrees it is contested:

- KOSPI 1y ago **3,226** → 52-week high **9,114** → today **6,912**
- **24% below its high**, having fallen 23% from three months ago and rallied 14.8% back in one month
- Three largest daily moves in two years: **+17.9%** (31 Jul 2026), +12.1%, +10.8%

Single sessions of ±18% on a major national index are not the behaviour of a market that has
settled on a price.

## The decision

**The trade already happened.** The correct response to discovering a 120pp opportunity cost is not
to buy the thing that produced it after it has doubled, at peak margins, 24% off its high, with
18% daily moves. That is the late-cycle error in its purest form, and it is the one that a
performance table like the one above is *designed* to provoke.

What the analysis does change:

1. **India's underperformance is real and mostly currency.** Any India case must now clear a
   currency hurdle explicitly, not implicitly. Quoting Indian returns in rupees, as this workspace
   has largely done, flatters them by roughly 7pp a year.
2. **Do not add to India on "it's cheap relative to its own history"** while the currency is
   underperforming its peer group. Those are two different arguments and only one of them is
   being tested.
3. **The memory cycle is the thing to track, not the Korean index.** If SK Hynix's margin rolls
   over from 71.5%, that is a global capex and electronics signal that reaches Indian EMS names
   directly — and it is the same BOM channel already flagged against Dixon.
4. **Korea deserves a watchlist entry with an entry condition, not an allocation.** The condition
   is margin *stabilisation* at a lower level with the multiple re-expanding, which is what the
   bottom of a memory cycle looks like. Not this.

## What would falsify this

If SK Hynix's next quarter holds above 70% operating margin *and* the forward multiple re-rates
above ~8×, the market has changed its mind about sustainability and the cyclical-top read is wrong.
Watch the multiple, not the margin — the margin is the thing everyone already sees.
