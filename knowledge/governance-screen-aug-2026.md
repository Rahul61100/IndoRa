# Governance screen: 8 of 9 AMBER, 1 CLEAN, 0 RED — and HAL is the one to act on

Verified 2026-08-27. **Confidence: REPORTED** — news-sourced, post-search-cap. Several items rest
on single sources and are flagged as such. **This is the check that never ran yesterday and whose
absence closed HDFC Bank this morning** ([[i-never-ran-governance-on-my-own-book]]).

**Ranked worst to cleanest: HAL · ICICI Bank · NTPC · Infosys · Reliance · Bharti · Dixon · SBI ·
L&T.**

The framing that matters most: **no name shows the specific HDFC triad firing at once — a
regulator questioning penalty adequacy, a self-dealing payment, and a US class action. But several
show the same underlying failure mode: governance signal accumulating in pieces while the desk
watches the operating metric.**

## HAL — highest concern, and far worse than I knew

I had "Tejas Mk1A two years late, MoD weighing penalties." The actual chain:

- **21 Nov 2025** — Tejas crashes at the Dubai Airshow, **pilot killed**. HAL calls it "isolated."
- **Feb 2026** — a second incident **grounds the Tejas fleet**. HAL calls it a "minor glitch."
- **Mar 2026** — traced to a software fault; fleet-wide upgrade ordered.
- **Jun 2026** — MoD weighing financial penalties, **zero Mk-1A jets delivered.**
- **Jun 2026** — **199 forged supplier test certificates** for Tejas components found by HAL's own
  audit. FIR filed.
- Recurring Parliamentary Standing Committee censure on inefficiencies and R&D delays.
- A February 2024 exchange notice for **insufficient independent directors** was never confirmed
  resolved.

**Forged test certificates on a fighter aircraft is not a delivery-schedule problem. It is a
quality-control governance failure**, and it explains the 7% revenue CAGR against a ₹94,000 crore
order book far better than "execution" does.

## ICICI Bank — the closest structural analog to the HDFC pattern

**And it is my strongest short-horizon position.**

- **Six GST demand notices in six months, over ₹1,350 crore combined** — ₹49cr, ₹216cr, ₹237.9cr,
  ₹50.4cr, then ₹769cr and ₹384cr together in March 2026
- **SEBI warning** over early FPI fund repatriation (May-June 2026); **RBI penalty ₹75 lakh**
  (Aug 2025)
- **The CEO reappointment thread is still open.** Bakhshi's extension reported Jan 2026 → activist
  Prashant Bhushan writes to the RBI opposing it → RBI approves anyway in May → **Bhushan escalates
  again on 24 August 2026, three days ago.**

Clean on auditor, promoter and securities litigation. But the shape — regulator actions
accumulating alongside a contested CEO reappointment — is the pattern worth naming.

## The rest, briefly

**NTPC** — a **live CBI FIR at Ramagundam**, ₹35 crore of falsified wage bills 2017-22, naming five
BHEL officials **plus unnamed NTPC personnel**. And a **CMD succession vacuum running 20+ months
through two failed PESB rounds**, now extended to January 2027.

**Infosys** — **the CFO exited with no stated reason on 19 August, eight days ago**, and his
predecessor Nilanjan Roy was reappointed the same day. Single-source, thin coverage. Separately,
CEO Salil Parekh **personally paid ₹25 lakh** in a 2024 SEBI insider-trading settlement. The
₹32,403 crore GST notice was fully closed in the company's favour; ₹415 crore and ₹82.75 crore
remain open.

**Reliance** — **CBI arrested a Reliance SVP** (Asteria Aerospace, the drone unit) alongside a DGCA
official in an April 2026 bribery probe. **SEBI issued an administrative warning** over employee
insider trading in a July 2024 UPSI window. A related-party sale via an OFCD structure is being
press-framed as "a test of SEBI's RPT rules." Offsetting: **zero promoter pledging**, and promoters
*increased* their stake.

**Bharti Airtel** — **the heaviest promoter-selling signal of the nine: Mittal entity plus Singtel
sold over $4bn across 2025**, each with a stated commercial rationale. The retrospective spectrum
charge relief (₹8,414 crore) is under Supreme Court appeal with **no stay** — still open.

**Dixon** — **JV partner Vivo India is under an active ED/SFIO money-laundering probe**; the
government approved the Dixon-Vivo JV under Press Note 3 anyway. Co-founder sold 2.77% for ₹2,221
crore. Analyst notes flag margin-quality stress by name. **One item conflicts with our own data:
the screen reports a 36% year-on-year net-profit drop, while our revision pull shows TTM profit
+119% and FY+1 EPS revised +10.07% over 90 days.** Unresolved — different periods or a bad figure.

**SBI** — **the most important catch in the whole screen, and it is a false-positive warning.** SBI
generates heavy CBI and ED headline volume from the Anil Ambani / RCom ₹2,929 crore fraud saga —
**but SBI is the party that detected and classified the fraud, and every court ruling has upheld
its action.** That is fraud control working: **the functional opposite of a governance flag.** A
keyword screen would have flagged it wrongly. Otherwise only a ₹6,337 crore tax demand under
appeal, routine at its scale.

**L&T — the only CLEAN name.** A ₹2,237 crore GST demand was *quashed in its favour*; ₹173 crore
remains under appeal. Ordinary EPC arbitration. No CCI, ED, CBI or SEBI action, no auditor issue,
no whistleblower, and no promoter in the traditional sense.

## Two contamination traps to guard against

- **An NTPC exchange penalty was reported by several outlets as "₹59 crore." The underlying BSE
  filing shows ~₹10.7 lakh** — a 550x error in circulation.
- **The "₹2,500 crore, India's first corporate class action" belongs to Jindal Polyfilms, not
  Reliance Industries**, despite headline conflation.

## What this changes

**Eight AMBERs is not eight problems — it is what a real governance screen looks like on nine large
Indian companies.** The value is in the ranking and in knowing which threads are live, not in
treating AMBER as a sell signal.

**The one that changes a position is HAL.** Related: [[hdfc-bank-msrdc-governance-overhang]].
