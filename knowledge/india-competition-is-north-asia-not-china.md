# India's flow competitor is the Korea-Taiwan semiconductor cycle, not China

Verified 2026-08-26. I framed the EM question as India versus China. **That framing is wrong, and
the real answer is worse for India.**

## MSCI EM weights, 31 July 2026

| Country | Weight |
|---|---|
| **Taiwan** | **26.63%** |
| **China** | **21.38%** |
| **South Korea** | **20.33%** |
| **India** | **11.66%** |
| Brazil | 4.15% |

**Taiwan and Korea together are ~47% of EM. India is fourth, at roughly half China's weight and
under half of Taiwan's or Korea's.** The mechanical index-flow math works against India before any
active allocation decision is made.

## The returns that caused it — and they are earnings, not re-rating

| Market | 1-year (local) |
|---|---|
| **KOSPI** | **+113.61%** (won ~flat, so USD similar) |
| **TAIEX** | +58% to +87% YTD (**sources disagree — unresolved**) |
| **Nikkei 225** | +55.06% |
| MSCI EM | +36.44% |
| MSCI China | **−0.98%** |
| **MSCI India** | **−6.49%** |

- **SK Hynix Q2 2026: operating profit +557% year on year, operating margin 76%**, revenue ~$55bn.
  H1 revenue topped ₩100tn for the first time. It holds **56-58% of global HBM revenue share** —
  **and trades under 4x forward earnings.**
- **TSMC raised FY2026 revenue growth guidance to "slightly above 40%"**, Q2 net income +77.4% to
  a record, capex guidance $60-64bn, two-thirds of revenue now HPC and AI.

**This is a real earnings cycle, not a multiple story** — which makes it a far harder flow magnet
to compete with than China's macro-repair narrative. It is the bigger structural threat to India's
pitch as "the growth alternative to China" than China itself is.

*(A strategist's caveat the data invites: sub-4x forward earnings on 76% operating margins is what
a cyclical peak looks like priced as a cyclical peak. The market is not mistaking this for a
secular business. That cuts both ways for anyone chasing it.)*

## What it has cost India, quantified

- **2026 FPI outflows are the worst yearly tally since 1993** — over ₹2.79 lakh crore by mid-June,
  including a record ₹1.17 lakh crore in March alone. July finally broke a four-month streak with
  a ₹17,227 crore inflow. One month is not a trend.
- **Foreign ownership of Indian stocks is at a 14-year low of 14.7%.**
- Cited drivers: rupee weakness, oil, strong dollar and high US yields, and **AI-led global capital
  concentration** — mostly *not* China-specific.

## The sell side has already moved

- **JPMorgan downgraded India to Neutral and cut its 2026 Nifty target 10% to 27,000**, explicitly
  citing "elevated valuations compared to emerging market peers", and pivoted toward **Asia tech**.
- **Goldman downgraded India to Market Weight.**
- **Jefferies: "70 large EM funds underweight India."**
- The only clean explicit China-over-India call found was UBS's — and it is a year stale.

## And China is not actually fixed

PPI turned positive in Q2 2026 (+4.1%, ending 41 months of deflation) — real progress. But CPI is
+0.5% and **decelerating**, H1 retail sales +1.3%, vehicle sales -21.1%, property still falling in
most tiers with Jan-May investment -16.2%, and tech regulation is **tightening** not easing. The
2026 growth target was set at **4.5-5% — below 5% for the first time.** The China re-rating case
rests on a policy put and PPI stabilisation, not on a consumer or property recovery.

**MSCI China trades at 10.97x forward against MSCI India's 20.32x — India carries 1.85x China's
multiple.**

## How to apply

Stop watching China as the India substitute. **Watch the Korea-Taiwan semiconductor cycle** — it
is where the EM money actually went, and the signal that frees capital to return to India is a
*slowdown* there, not a China recovery. KOSPI's July 2026 bear-market dip is the first hint that
such a rotation is even possible.

**The rotation-back-to-India checklist:** a 2026-dated flow print showing sustained India inflows;
a fresh dated comparative note naming India over China (reversing JPM's April downgrade); India's
own earnings actually delivering ([[CORRECTION-india-earnings-growth-was-4-5-percent]] says the
base rate is poor); and profit-taking in the North Asia semi trade.
