# A short-horizon trade must beat a long hold by 10-12% gross, before it earns its place

Verified 2026-08-27. **I have been recommending "few months" Indian equity trades with zero tax
accounting. This quantifies what that omission was worth, and it is not small.**

## The rates (Budget 2026 changed none of them)

| | Rate | Holding | Effective |
|---|---|---|---|
| **STCG** (§111A) | **20%** flat, **no exemption at all** | ≤12 months | 23 Jul 2024 |
| **LTCG** (§112A) | **12.5%** flat, no indexation | >12 months | 23 Jul 2024 |
| **LTCG annual exemption** | **₹1,25,000** | LTCG bucket only | 23 Jul 2024 |
| Surcharge on §111A/112/112A and dividends | **capped at 15%** — the 25% and 37% slabs are statutorily disapplied | | standing |
| Cess | 4% on tax + surcharge | | standing |

All-in: **STCG 20.8% → 23.92%**, **LTCG 13.0% → 14.94%** depending on surcharge.

**Budget 2026 held every one of these unchanged.** Pre-Budget commentary speculating about an LTCG
cut to 10%, a higher exemption or restored indexation **did not happen** — any note still assuming
it is wrong.

## The arithmetic

```
required gross-return ratio = (1 − LTCG effective) / (1 − STCG effective)
                            = (1 − 0.130) / (1 − 0.208)
                            = 0.870 / 0.792 = 1.0985
```

**A trade held under 12 months must earn ~9.85% more gross return than a >12-month hold just to
deliver the same rupee after tax.** For a high-income investor (both surcharge-capped at 15%):
**≈11.8%**.

On ₹1,00,000 at a 10% gross gain: short nets **₹7,670**, long nets **₹8,450**. To match, the short
trade needs **~10.98%** rather than 10%.

**And two multipliers make it worse:**

1. **The exemption asymmetry.** LTCG has a **₹1.25 lakh annual tax-free band. STCG has none —
   taxed from rupee one.** For gains inside that band the wedge is not 9.85%, it is **infinite**:
   0% against 20.8%.
2. **Turnover.** The wedge above is one short trade against one long trade. A strategy round-tripping
   3-4 times to cover the period a long holder sits through once pays STT and brokerage **3-4 times
   over** — roughly 0.5-0.75pp of additional drag at ~0.25% per round trip.

## A correction to my own reasoning

**I assumed the February 2026 STT hike penalised short-horizon equity trades. It does not.**

The hike was **F&O only** — futures **0.02% → 0.05%** (a 150% rise) and options on premium
**0.10% → 0.15%**, effective 1 April 2026. **Cash-market delivery (0.1% + 0.1%) and intraday STT
are unchanged**, and the Income Tax Department issued a clarification saying exactly that.

**And STT does not discriminate by holding period anyway** — the same round-trip cost applies
whether a position is held three months or three years. It matters only through *turnover*.

So the STT hike is **a red herring for the horizon question**. The tax-rate wedge is the real
reason, and it predates the hike by eighteen months. I would have cited the wrong cause.

## What this does to the book

**The SHORT bucket now has an explicit hurdle it was never held to.** A short-horizon idea must
carry an expected gross edge of **at least 10-12% over the best available long-horizon
alternative** — not merely "look attractive."

That is a demanding bar, and it means short-horizon positions should be **high-conviction,
asymmetric and event-driven** — a dated catalyst with a real re-rating attached — rather than
routine positioning with a modest edge. **Anything with a modest edge is better expressed as a
long-horizon add, or skipped.**

Note how badly this would have gone unexamined: the Bharti thesis was a pure event trade whose
event has stalled ([[session-2026-08-27-india]]). Under this framework it needed roughly 10-12%
of edge *over simply owning something for a year* to justify existing at all.

## Three things that changed while I was not looking

**Buybacks flipped twice in under two years, and my note is stale.**
- Pre-Oct 2024: taxed at company level, largely tax-free to shareholders
- **Oct 2024 – Mar 2026: the entire buyback amount taxed as dividend at slab** — worse than simply
  selling in the market
- **Budget 2026, effective April 2026: reverted to capital-gains treatment for non-promoters** —
  tax only on the actual gain. Buybacks are broadly tax-neutral against a market sale again.
  Promoters face an additional layer (~22% domestic / ~30% others).

**REITs and InvITs got materially more tax-efficient — twice.**
- July 2024: LTCG holding period cut **3 years → 1 year**, aligning with equity
- **Early August 2026 — two weeks ago:** the Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act 2026 makes
  **unit-holders exempt on the dividend component of distributions**, with the cost absorbed at
  SPV level via a surcharge rise from 10% to 25%
- Still taxable: the **interest component** (slab) and **return of capital above issue price**
  under §56(2)(xii). "Not all REIT distributions are tax-free" headlines exist to correct exactly
  that overreading.

I flagged REITs and InvITs as an ignored asset class. **There is now a tax reason to stop ignoring
them**, independent of the yield case.

**Losses: STCL offsets both STCG and LTCG; LTCL offsets only LTCG.** Eight-year carry-forward —
**but only if the return is filed by the original due date.**
