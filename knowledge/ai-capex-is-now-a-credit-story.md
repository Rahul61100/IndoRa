# The AI trade has split — chips still work, the debt-funded buildout does not

Verified 2026-08-26. The single label "AI trade" now hides two groups moving in opposite
directions.

**Still working — the supply-constrained hardware:**
- Micron **+702.9% over a year** on the memory shortage. Goldman calls it the worst supply
  shortage in 15 years: DRAM short 4.9%, NAND 4.2%, **HBM 5.1%**. DRAM prices rose ~90% in Q1
  2026. **Micron's HBM capacity is sold out through CY2026** under multi-year contracts.
- The AI-Semis basket is **+104% over a year with 88% breadth** — the best group in the universe.
- But note the drawdowns: MU is 23% below its high, MRVL 24%, AMD 17%, COHR 33%. Three-month
  return is **-1.2%**, median RSI 48.9. A parabolic year that is now consolidating, not extending.

**Broken — the debt-funded buildout:**
- **Oracle -37.2% over a year**, more than 50% off its record, its worst week since the 2001
  dot-com bust. Free cash flow deficit widened to **-$23.7bn** from -$394mn a year earlier; it
  plans to raise **$40bn** in FY27 after $43bn of debt and $5bn of equity in FY26. The fear is
  that its largest AI customers cannot meet their obligations.
- Our AI-Datacenter-Power basket is **-8.9% over a year and -18.1% over three months** with 44%
  breadth. VRT -30% from its high, TLN near its 52-week low.
- The nuclear/SMR leg has collapsed outright: **SMR -73.8%, OKLO -39.6%** over a year, and the
  speculative Space/Quantum basket is **-46.3% over three months with 0% breadth.**
- Morgan Stanley expects AI-related global debt issuance to **more than double to ~$570bn in
  2026**, with hyperscaler spending above **$1trn by 2027**.

**Why it matters:** with the 30-year at 5.2% ([[fed-may-hike-next]]), the market has stopped
paying for AI capex and started underwriting it. Whoever funds the buildout with debt is now
priced as a credit; whoever *sells into* the buildout with constrained supply is still priced as
a grower.

**How to apply:** never treat "AI exposure" as one factor. Separate by who takes the balance-sheet
risk. And do not read the semis' 104% year as a live entry — the group is consolidating 20-30%
below its highs with a flat three-month return.
