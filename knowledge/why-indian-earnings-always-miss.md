# Why Indian consensus misses every year — three causes, and one is a measurement artifact

Verified 2026-08-27. **Confidence: mixed — flagged per claim.** Four earlier passes confirmed the
nine-year miss and none explained it. This is the explanation, and it is not one thing.

## Cause 1 — sell-side optimism sets the starting point too high (SUPPORTED)

**The rating distribution has stopped doing discriminating work.** HDFC Bank carries **40 Buy /
1 Hold / 0 Sell across 41 analysts** while trading at 52-week lows amid a governance probe, an FII
exodus and NIM at a record low. A market that cannot produce a single Sell on that setup is not
producing information.

There is an academic literature: an Indian study on analyst optimism bias going back to the
2004-2007 post-reform window, and a China analogue finding optimism bias is **"associated with
higher brokerage trading volume"** — optimistic forecasts are *commercially rewarded* through
order flow. The mechanism is structural, not incidental.

**But optimism alone cannot explain a *widening* miss.** A stable bias produces a stable gap.

## Cause 2 — a few large cyclical sectors do most of the damage (SUPPORTED)

**The miss is concentrated, not broad-based.** FY26 cumulative downgrades to the full-year estimate
ran **Q1 −2%, Q2 −4%, Q3 −3%, Q4 −6%** — about **−15% cumulative** before a small +2% net upgrade.

Within that: **Telecom +30%, Oil & Gas +13%, PSU Banks +5%** upgraded; **Utilities −8%, Autos −3%,
Healthcare −3%** cut. In the prior cycle it was **Autos, Cement, Oil & Gas and NBFCs** cut hardest.

**Oil & Gas appears on both sides in different periods** — a commodity and refining-margin-driven
sector whose index weight lets it swing the aggregate on its own.

**The consequence matters: the median company is closer to plan than the index number implies.**
The headline miss overstates the typical forecasting error.

This is also where the *widening* comes from — the recurring, recurringly wrong narrative that
"this time margins normalise" in exactly the most cyclical sectors. Banks are the live example:
analysts modelled credit costs normalising and instead got ageing provisions, unsecured stress and
microfinance, with FY26 bank earnings growth potentially halving to ~6.5%.

## Cause 3 — part of the "miss" is survivorship bias, not forecasting error (REASONED, not sourced)

**This is the insight I had never considered, and it changes how the number should be read.**

FY26 growth is reported as **4.5% actual but "6.9% on the reconstituted index."** I had taken that
as a footnote. It is not.

**A "reconstituted" figure recomputes history using TODAY's constituents applied retroactively.**
The Nifty is reconstituted semi-annually, and companies are removed **disproportionately because
they deteriorated** — removal is lagging confirmation of a miss.

So the reconstituted number **silently drops the losers and backfills with names that are in the
index today precisely because they performed.** It is survivorship-biased upward. **The 4.5%, using
the basket that actually existed, is the honest, investable number.**

And the mirror image is the real point: **consensus was built bottom-up on a basket that included
future removals, and nobody re-bases the original estimate when a name is dropped for cause.** Part
of what is reported as "consensus was wrong" is "the index membership changed and the comparison
was never re-based."

**Flagged as reasoned inference — the methodology note defining "reconstituted" could not be
retrieved.** But if correct, the headline 7-12pp miss **overstates the pure forecasting
component**.

## How the three fit together

- **Optimism** sets the level too high every year — necessary, not sufficient
- **Cyclical-sector margin error** explains why the gap has *widened* over four years
- **Reconstitution** deflates the headline without reducing the company-level error

## What to do differently — six rules

1. **Apply a standing 6-8pp haircut to January consensus, mechanically, every year, regardless of
   that year's narrative.** If consensus says 14-15%, plan around mid-single digits. FY25 and FY26
   delivered 3.4% and 4.5%.
2. **Discount hardest in Autos, Cement, Oil & Gas/OMCs, NBFCs and bank credit-cost lines.** Apply
   little or none to Telecom, Metals and PSU Banks while they carry upgrades. **The miss is
   concentrated — do not haircut uniformly.**
3. **Trade the revision, not the level.** The level is untrustworthy; the *change* is informative.
   This is exactly what [[revision-breadth-is-now-collected]] now measures daily, and it validates
   building it.
4. **Treat Buy ratings as near-uninformative in India.** **Rating *changes* carry far more
   information than rating *levels*** — a downgrade in a market this reluctant to downgrade is a
   real signal. JM Financial cutting HDFC Bank to ₹800 mattered; the other 40 Buys did not.
5. **Always ask which basket an index-earnings-growth figure used** — current constituents applied
   retroactively, or the basket that actually existed. Default to the latter.
6. **The widening is the more urgent question than the existence.** A stable bias can be priced in;
   an intensifying one cannot. Whether it is worsening sell-side incentives, macro deterioration
   outpacing model updates, or post-COVID base effects rolling off — **unresolved, and it is the
   next research pass.**

## What could not be established

- **A market-wide Buy/Hold/Sell histogram** for the Nifty 500 over the nine-year window — the
  single number that would make cause 1 load-bearing rather than illustrative.
- **Whether India is an outlier in magnitude** versus China, Brazil or Indonesia. The *mechanism*
  is shared — the China paper shows it — but no side-by-side comparison was reachable. **India may
  not be special; it may just be the market I am looking at.**
- The NSE Corporate Earnings Review PDFs, which carry the margin-line and same-constituent detail,
  **timed out rather than being blocked** — worth retrying with the cookie-handshake method that
  worked for the participant-OI file ([[fii-are-unwinding-the-long-leg]]).
