# The monsoon is the biggest India trade of the last six months and my book has no position on it

A first-order macro fact absent from everything written in this workspace until today: **India's
2026 monsoon is running 13–15% below normal — the weakest since 2009 — with a strengthening
"super" El Niño.** Skymet put 2026 drought odds at **70%**. As of 16 July, 111 districts were
flagged El Niño-vulnerable and **69 were already rainfall-deficient**.

For an economy where agriculture is a modest share of GDP but a large share of *employment* and an
enormous share of *consumption breadth*, that is not a sector story. It is the demand backdrop.

## The market priced it months ago

Performance relative to the Nifty, six months:

**A 21.4pp spread.** That is the single largest persistent divergence visible anywhere in the
Indian market right now, and it holds across three independent expressions of the same theme —
FMCG, fertiliser/agrochemical, and tractors — which is what separates a real macro signal from a
sector rotation.

Two rural names buck it: **Hero +2.8pp and M&M +2.1pp**. Both have large non-rural mix now (premium
two-wheelers, SUVs), which is the exception that supports the reading rather than undermining it.

## What this exposes about my own book

Seven open positions: ICICI, SBI, NTPC, Reliance, Bharti, L&T (benched), Infosys (benched).

**Not one of them expresses either side of the dominant trend in the market I am researching.** And
the one with the clearest macro linkage sits on the wrong side of it — the power complex is
**−7.1pp** relative (NTPC −7.8pp, Power Grid −7.1pp, Tata Power −1.4pp), despite drought normally
meaning more irrigation pumping and higher cooling load. That the power names did *not* benefit is
itself information: either the linkage is weaker than assumed, or something else is dominating the
regulated-utility complex.

This is the kind of gap that does not show up in any screen I had built, because every screen I
built looked at individual securities. **A dominant cross-sectional spread is invisible to a
security-level process.** That is a structural blind spot, not an oversight.

## Two cautions against over-reading it

1. **Attribution is not clean.** FMCG has taken a **$5.2bn FII outflow over twelve straight
   months** (independently confirmed), and ITC and Britannia are at 52-week lows. Some of the rural
   underperformance is a flows story about one sector rather than a monsoon story about the
   economy. The fertiliser and tractor names moving the same way is what argues for the monsoon
   reading, but it does not prove it.
2. **The winning side is not obviously investable.** Eternal is the single best performer at
   **+38.8pp** — and it also carries two earnings-quality flags from today's screen: revenue
   +246.5% with operating income **negative** at −₹215cr a year ago, and net income at 1.92×
   operating income. The urban-premium trade is partly momentum on businesses that do not yet make
   money. **Chasing +38.8pp of relative performance into a loss-making quick-commerce name is not
   the lesson here.**

## What to actually do with it

- **Do not add rural-exposed consumption** on "it's cheap now". A 70%-probability drought is a
  reason for it to be cheap, and the rainfall deficit was still widening at the last reading.
- **The tradeable version, if any, is the second-order one**: food inflation from a deficient
  monsoon pushes CPI up, which locks the RBI out of cutting. This workspace already holds that
  [[rate-cut-cycle-is-over|the rate-cut cycle is over]] — the monsoon is a *mechanism* for that,
  and it strengthens a conclusion reached on other grounds.
- **Watch the deficit close.** A monsoon deficit that narrows through September flips this trade
  hard and fast, because the rural complex is priced for the bad outcome. That makes it a dated,
  falsifiable setup rather than a view — which is the only kind worth holding.
- **Add a cross-sectional spread monitor to the daily loop.** The process missed a 21.4pp
  divergence for months because nothing in it looks at groups against each other.

**Source:** relative performance computed here from own price pulls. Monsoon deficit, Skymet drought

**not** verified against an IMD primary release — that check is outstanding and the number is
load-bearing enough to deserve it.
