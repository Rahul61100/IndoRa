# This regime is a splice of three, and the breadth signature is what says "not yet a top"

Research completed 2026-08-26. **The most useful strategic frame the workspace has produced.**

## The finding

There is no single historical analog. The present is **three regimes running simultaneously**:

1. **A 1994-style macro backdrop** — a hawkish Fed *holding* into resilient growth over the
   objection of its own hawks, long-end stress (30y at 5.2%), no recession priced.
2. **A 1999-2000 and 2006-07 style structure underneath** — a debt-financed capex supercycle
   ([[ai-capex-is-now-a-credit-story]]) plus credit bifurcation, with investment-grade spreads at
   historic lows while the bottom of the stack deteriorates ([[private-credit-is-the-real-us-risk]]).
3. **Genuinely broadening equity participation, which neither prior top had.**

## The discriminator, and it is the single most important line here

**In both 1999-2000 and 2006-07 breadth was NARROWING for months before the top** — the
advance/decline line diverged from price, fewer new highs as the index rose. That was the
signature of both distribution tops.

**Today's breadth is broadening** — 64% of US names above their 200 DMA and rising, healthcare at
100% member breadth, small caps leading ([[us-leadership-rotated-to-value-health-smallcap]]).
That is close to the opposite signature.

**So the equity tape does not yet look like a top.** But note what that means precisely: the
breadth signal and the credit signal are pointing in **opposite directions**, and historically
they move together. **That divergence is itself the notable fact — more than either signal alone.**
It is not a stable equilibrium and it resolves one way or the other.

## Lead times, from the historical record

**Bottom-tier credit stress to broad market stress: 6-18 months**, with real variance and real
false positives. The discriminator between outcomes:
- **2015-16 energy HY** — CCC E&P spreads blew out to 1,500-2,000bp; a real bankruptcy wave (35
  producers, $82bn of filed debt by 2016) that stayed **contained**, because it was one sector's
  direct lenders.
- **2006-07 subprime** — bottom-tier tranches cracked Feb-Jun 2007 while IG stayed tight into that
  summer. Equity top came ~2-3 months later; the systemic event ~13 months later.

**Private credit today is structurally closer to the 2007 case** — broadly distributed, opaque,
held across insurers, pensions and BDCs — rather than the 2015-16 case. That is why it deserves
more weight than "one sector had a bad year".

**Peak capex to credit event: 12-30 months.**

| Case | Peak | Trigger | Credit event | Lag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Telecom | 1999-2000 | traffic demand never came | WorldCom bankruptcy, Jul 2002 | ~24-30 mo |
| US shale | 2011-14 | OPEC Nov 2014 price war | 35 producers, $82bn filed debt by 2016 | ~12-24 mo |
| UK railways | ~1845 | BoE hikes drained credit | 1847 panic | ~24-36 mo |

The trigger is always a **demand shock or a financing shock**, never the capex failing
operationally in the interim. AI capex is 2-3 years into its ramp, so the window sits roughly
**2026-2028**. Oracle is the specific canary — financing capex it cannot cover from operating cash
flow is the exact profile that preceded both prior events.

## India's real analog is 2017-18, not any US year

SEBI's October 2017 mutual-fund recategorisation plus SIP-driven retail flows pushed mid and
small caps to an extreme premium over large caps. What followed was a **small-cap-specific
correction of roughly 40-60% peak to trough into 2019-20, while the Nifty large-cap index merely
chopped sideways.**

That maps directly onto today: smid at a two-sigma premium ([[india-smid-premium-is-two-sigma]]),
a record SIP bid and a ₹4.72 trillion IPO pipeline ([[india-supply-versus-the-sip-bid]]).
**If India resolves badly, the damage should concentrate in small and mid caps and the IPO
pipeline, not in large caps.** That is an argument for the existing large-cap discipline.

## Analogs explicitly rejected — false analogies are worse than none

- **1970s stagflation.** A real stagflation shows broad commodity strength *including energy*.
  Brent is 27% *below* its high while gold, silver and copper are strong. That is a monetary and
  capex story, not a supply-shock inflation.
- **2021-22.** That was the *start* of a hiking cycle that crushed stocks and bonds together. The
  big repricing already happened; VIX 15.5 and broadening breadth are incompatible.
- **Japan 1989.** The opposite breadth structure — only ~25% free float, keiretsu cross-holding
  inflating a narrow float. Keep one thread though: **AI's circular vendor financing rhymes with
  keiretsu cross-holding** as a concentration-of-counterparty-risk warning.
- **India 1992** (a settlement fraud) and **India 2008** (a global crash) — neither is a
  valuation-divergence story.

## Midterm base rate, distribution not average

Since 1950, 19 midterm years: average S&P return **+5.9%**, positive in only **11 of 19 (58%)**,
and an **average intra-year drawdown of -17.5%**. The distribution is bimodal — a rough year with
a deep pullback, then resolution. The robust part: **the S&P has been higher 12 months after every
midterm since 1950 — 19 for 19, averaging +15.4%.** A choppy 2026 into a strong 2027 is the
*normal* midterm shape, not a forecast.

## What would falsify this frame

**Toward benign:** CCC-B compresses back under ~450bp without recession; private credit default
rate rolls over from 6.0%; breadth keeps rising; Fed dissents flip from hikes to cuts.

**Toward distribution top:** **breadth begins narrowing while headline indices hold** — the exact
signature present in 2000 and 2007 and absent today; IG spreads start widening in sympathy with
CCC-B instead of staying decoupled; a second AI-capex name follows Oracle into a debt-funded FCF
deficit; VIX above 20 while breadth is still falling.

**For India:** whether a small-cap correction stays contained to small caps (2017-18 pattern) or
drags the large-cap multiple down with it despite +18% earnings.
