# The memory supercycle has a victim side — and it lands on Indian phone assembly

Verified 2026-08-26. **This is the second-order the semis story hides, and it reaches the open
book.**

The upstream side is well covered: worst memory shortage in 15 years, HBM sold out through
CY2026, Micron +702.9% ([[ai-capex-is-now-a-credit-story]]). The downstream side is not.

Gartner: combined DRAM and SSD prices up **130% by end-2026**, pushing **PC prices +17% and
smartphone prices +13%** against 2025. Consequence:

- **PC shipments -10.4% and smartphone shipments -8.4% in 2026** — the steepest device
  contraction in over a decade
- Premium handsets absorb it on margin. **Entry and mid-tier Android take the hit**, because
  memory is a large share of the bill of materials there and a headline marketing spec
- Brands are responding by **raising launch prices and downgrading specs**, and turning cautious
  on production plans as demand softens
- Buyers are extending replacement cycles or going refurbished

**Why it matters to India, directly:** this is a live risk to the **Dixon** thesis in
`positions/open-theses.md`. Dixon assembles smartphones, weighted to exactly the mid- and low-end
Android segment where memory is the largest BOM swing and demand is most price-elastic. A
contract assembler earning roughly 3% margins has no room to absorb a BOM shock and little
pricing power to pass one on, and it is paid per unit — so an 8.4% shipment contraction hits
volume and margin at the same time.

That risk was **not** in the thesis when it was written. The thesis was built on the ₹1.9 lakh
crore policy push (Semicon 2.0 plus Mobile PLI 2.0) and on 100% EMS sector breadth. Both remain
true. The policy tailwind and the BOM headwind now have to be weighed against each other.

**How to apply:** before adding to Dixon, check its BOM exposure and whether contracts pass
component cost through. Watch smartphone shipment data as a direct input, not background colour.
More generally — **whenever a shortage makes an upstream supplier a star, ask who is paying for
it downstream, and whether we own them.** Related: [[cross-market-lead-lag-is-the-edge]].
