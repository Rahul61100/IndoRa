# India sector map — the eight sectors I had never looked at

Verified 2026-08-26. Coverage fill-in. Prices as at that date.

## Where price and fundamentals visibly disagree

**Life insurance — the clearest dislocation found.** HDFC Life at **₹553, −28.8% over a year and
at a two-year low**, SBI Life −2.7% but −15.1% over six months. Yet **SBI Life's APE grew 36% year
on year in Q1 FY27** and industry life APE is **+15% YoY**, with VNB margins holding in the
mid-20s (HDFC Life 25.0%, SBI Life 26.2%, guiding 26-28%). The real damage is a **50-100bp margin
drag** from GST 2.0 — individual life and health premiums were exempted from 18% GST in September
2025, but insurers **lost the input tax credit**.

A ~60bp margin hit does not explain a 29% de-rating. This reads as **FII-liquidity de-rating
hitting a business whose growth engine has not broken** — consistent with foreign ownership at a
14-year low of 14.7% ([[india-competition-is-north-asia-not-china]]). **Worth working name by
name.** Star Health is the exception, +34.2% over a year.

**Specialty chemicals is two unrelated stories under one label, and the market has already sorted
them.**
- **Fluorochemicals HAVE turned** — Navin Fluorine **+43.75% YTD**, SRF running Q1 FY27 at 100%
  utilisation with management saying pricing is "now holding out and getting better", Deepak
  Nitrite's MD reporting **Chinese capacity additions have stopped under new environmental
  restrictions**. That is a **supply-side structural shift, not a demand recovery** — which makes
  it more durable.
- **Agrochem has NOT** — PI Industries **−22.94% YTD**, Q1 export volumes −8%, revenue −10%, **PAT
  −39% YoY**, management unable to give a turn date.
- Note the ownership: **FII stakes in SRF and PI have fallen steadily since Sep 2023 while DII
  stakes rose by the same amount.** Domestic money is driving the fluorochemicals re-rating.

## Where valuation leaves no room

**Hospitals.** Apollo at 34.4x EV/EBITDA, Max at 44.1x, Dr Lal at 57.8x P/E — against a
**Parliamentary Standing Committee report tabled 7 August 2026 recommending private-hospital room
rents be capped at three-star-hotel rates, plus a national price-cap framework for essential
procedures and diagnostics.** Brokers dismissed it as noise and the index fell 3%. At these
multiples there is **no room for that proposal to advance.** Structural driver is real —
a ~100,000-bed shortfall — but the price already has it.

**Cement — consolidating into a two-horse race, and pricing power still absent.** UltraTech crossed
**200 MTPA in April 2026, a year early, now the largest outside China**, targeting 240 MTPA by
FY28 on ~₹16,000 crore capex. Adani (Ambuja/ACC) targets 140 MTPA by 2028 after Sanghi, Penna and
Orient. JSW explicitly opted out. **But price hikes are failing to stick** — a South India price
war plus rising petcoke costs. Consolidation plus heavy incoming capacity from both leaders is a
classic overcapacity setup. UltraTech −7.1% YTD, **Ambuja −26.8%**, Shree −17.4%.

## Where the public market is repricing a private-market standard

**Quick commerce still makes no money anywhere.** Blinkit's segment EBIT margin remains negative
with capex per new store roughly **doubled to ~₹2.5 crore**; Swiggy Instamart at −0.2%
contribution margin; **Zepto's FY26 net loss widened to ₹5,905 crore** even as revenue doubled.
Discounting of 13-20% is still the main acquisition lever.

**And Zepto postponed its IPO in late July 2026 over a valuation gap with public investors**,
pivoting to a ~₹1,000 crore pre-IPO placement, having last raised at **$7bn**. Meanwhile
**Flipkart Minutes has overtaken Instamart in dark-store count across the top 10 cities.**

That is the live signal: **the "growth now, profits later" standard that private rounds accepted
in 2021-24 is not being accepted by public markets in 2026.** A category-wide repricing in real
time, not one delayed listing.

## Other sectors, briefly

- **Renewables:** India crossed **300 GW of non-fossil capacity in mid-August 2026**, 60% of the
  500 GW 2030 target. ALMM-II domestic-cell mandate effective 1 June 2026 pushed solar
  manufacturing to ~172 GW — but **domestic cell supply now lags module demand**, adding ~₹15,000
  to a residential system, and India is probing Chinese-cell circumvention routed via Ethiopia.
  Order books: Suzlon 6.1 GW, Inox Wind 4.7 GW, Waaree ~₹61,500 crore. All four names are **down
  over a year** despite universal broker Buys — analyst-driven, not flow-driven.
- **Hotels, travel, QSR:** IndiGo posted a **₹238 crore Q1 FY27 net loss on ATF up ~80%**, yet hit
  an 8-month high days later. Indian Hotels 48.4x, Jubilant 89.3x. Broker enthusiasm running ahead
  of realised returns.
- **Capital markets:** covered separately in [[nse-ipo-is-the-supply-event-that-matters]].

**How to apply:** the two names worth actual work from this sweep are **life insurance** (price
and fundamentals genuinely disagree) and **fluorochemicals** (a supply-side structural turn the
market is paying for but which is not yet consensus outside DIIs). Everything else here is either
correctly priced or priced for perfection.
