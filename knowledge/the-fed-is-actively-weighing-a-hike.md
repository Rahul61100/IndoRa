# The Fed is not "on hold" — it is actively weighing a hike, and 15 September is the date

Verified 2026-08-26. I had this as "held five meetings, three dissents". **It is more live than
that.**

- The **28-29 July 2026 FOMC held 9-3 at 3.50-3.75%**, and the three dissenters — **Beth Hammack
  (Cleveland), Neel Kashkari (Minneapolis) and Lorie Logan (Dallas)** — each wanted a **25bp
  hike**, not a hold.
- **On 26 August 2026 the New York Times ran: "'Honeymoon's Over': Warsh Under Pressure as Fed
  Weighs Raising Rates."** Warsh is under pressure as the committee contemplates **tightening** —
  the opposite of what he was nominated to deliver.
- **Kevin Warsh was confirmed 54-45 on 13/22 May 2026 — the narrowest Fed-chair vote in history**,
  with a single Democrat voting yes. He pledged to be "strictly independent" and explicitly
  rejected cutting because the president wanted it.
- Bond markets were already pricing a hawkish risk premium into the long end ahead of his Jackson
  Hole debut. The **10-year closed 4.73% on 24 August**.

**Remaining 2026 FOMC dates: 15-16 September (with a Summary of Economic Projections), 27-28
October, 8-9 December.**

**15 September is a double catalyst.** The FOMC meeting opens the same day the **Senate holds its
cloture vote on the CLARITY Act** ([[crypto-regulation-timeline-2026]]) — Kalshi prices roughly
20% odds. Two separate, dated events with real market consequence on one day.

**The independence question is genuinely two-sided, which surprised me.** Trump's attempt to remove
Governor Lisa Cook has been **repeatedly blocked by the Supreme Court**, which treated the Fed
differently from the FTC in the parallel *Trump v. Slaughter* removal-power case (29 June 2026,
which reversed *Humphrey's Executor* for the FTC). Cook remains in office. Meanwhile the FT
frames **Bessent's own yield-suppression actions** — heavier bill issuance and doubled long-bond
buybacks, minimum per-operation $2bn to $4bn effective 9 September — as themselves a risk to Fed
credibility, not only the president's rhetoric.

Treasury bills are now **~22.2% of $31.4tn marketable debt**, above the conventional 15-20% TBAC
range. Effectiveness is contested: one Bloomberg piece reports the buybacks showing "early
effectiveness", another quotes Alyce Andres that Bessent "cannot control inflation expectations
nor force nominal long rates down".

**How to apply:** stop describing the Fed as on hold. The distribution has a hike in it, and the
market is pricing that into the long end already. **Any thesis in any market that assumes a stable
or falling US front end is fighting a live committee.** For India specifically, a US hike would
add a fourth simultaneous pressure on the rupee alongside oil, the lost Russian discount and the
term premium ([[india-in-usd-is-the-return-that-matters]]).
