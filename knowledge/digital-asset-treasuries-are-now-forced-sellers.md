# The digital-asset treasury companies have flipped from marginal buyer to latent forced seller

Verified 2026-08-26. **A structural risk I had entirely missed**, and it is the most important
unappreciated thing in crypto right now.

## The flywheel has broken

The DAT model worked by issuing equity above net asset value and buying coins with the proceeds —
accretive as long as the stock traded at a premium to the assets. **That premium is gone.**

**Strategy (MSTR):**
- Holds **840,447-842,137 BTC — about 4.0% of all bitcoin** — at an average cost of **~$75,391**
- **mNAV has collapsed from ~3.4x in November 2024 to ~0.72-0.74x on a market-cap basis** (~1.00x
  on enterprise value). It is trading at a **discount** on the basis that matters for the flywheel.
- Stock at $93.04, **down ~80% from its 2025 peak**
- ~$18.4-21.7bn of debt and preferred outstanding, against $4.8bn cash

**And it is already selling.** The board authorised a "Digital Credit Capital Framework" permitting
**up to $5bn of BTC sales**, and Strategy **sold 3,588 BTC in July 2026 specifically to fund
preferred-stock dividends.** Senior dividend claims on roughly $15bn of preferred are a live,
non-hypothetical forced-seller lever — this is no longer an equity-only structure.

**It is not just Strategy.** Satsuma, Smarter Web Company, Sequans, Nakamoto and Empery Digital
have all sold BTC in 2026 to service debt or operations. **Semler Scientific has fully exited** and
is now listed as a "Former Bitcoin Holder". Most DAT stocks trade **below 1.0x mNAV** — SharpLink
0.82x, Metaplanet 0.87x, Twenty One 0.74-1.25x depending on basis.

**Scale of the overhang:** roughly **200 companies hold ~1.264-1.28M BTC, about $99-100bn** — well
short of the $250bn year-end target the sector was pitching.

**The feedback loop analysts now flag:** forced selling → lower coin price → lower mNAV → more
forced selling.

## Why it matters

This cohort was a **price-insensitive marginal buyer on the way up**. Below 1.0x mNAV it becomes a
**price-insensitive marginal seller on the way down**, and it holds over 6% of bitcoin between
them — comparable to the entire spot ETF complex.

**Combined with the other two findings**, the crypto picture is coherent and it is not bullish:
the rally is a short squeeze ([[the-btc-bounce-was-a-short-squeeze]]), the native dollar base is
shrinking ([[stablecoin-supply-peaked-in-may]]), and the largest non-ETF holder cohort has an
incentive to sell. **Only ETF flows point the other way.**

**How to apply:** track MSTR's mNAV as a crypto risk indicator, not as a stock. Below ~0.8x on a
market-cap basis the forced-selling incentive strengthens. **No position taken, and this is now a
reason not to take one.**
