# India's MSCI EM weight has roughly halved, and it now trails EM by 42 points in a year

Verified 2026-08-26. The flow evidence behind [[india-in-usd-is-the-return-that-matters]], and it
is worse than the currency numbers alone suggested.

**Dollar returns to 31 July 2026, all from the same MSCI factsheet vintage so directly comparable:**

| | 1 year | 3y ann. | 5y ann. | 10y ann. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MSCI India** | **−5.34%** | +6.39% | +6.15% | +8.51% |
| **MSCI EM** | **+37.06%** | +19.89% | +8.52% | +9.64% |
| **MSCI World** | **+20.88%** | +18.64% | +11.70% | +13.29% |

**India trails MSCI EM by roughly 42 percentage points over one year and MSCI World by 26.** Over
three years it still trails EM by ~13.5pp annualised.

**Ownership has moved accordingly. India's weight in MSCI EM:**
- **~19.4-21%** at the Aug-Sep 2024 peak
- **16.21%** around Sep 2025 (a "two-year low" when reported)
- **~11.9-12%** at the most recent sourced print (May 2026 — an August figure was not obtainable)

**Roughly halved from the peak, and India has slipped from second-largest EM constituent to
fourth — behind China, Taiwan and Korea**, as capital rotated into the AI and semiconductor cycle.

**And India is still the most expensive major EM by a wide margin:** roughly 20-22x forward
against Brazil 8-10x, Korea 10-12x, China 10-13x, Taiwan 14-16x. Goldman notes India's premium to
global and EM has compressed below its own 10-year average — but compressed is not closed.

**Why it matters:** "cheap versus its own history" and "cheap versus the opportunity set" are
different claims, **and only the first is currently true.** A global EM allocator deciding where
to put new money is comparing India's multiple to Taiwan's AI-cycle earnings momentum, not to
India's own 2020 trough. That is the comparison the money is actually making, and the weight data
says it has been answering "not India" for two years.

**How to apply:** track India's MSCI EM weight as a flow indicator — it is coincident, not
lagging, and it is still pointed down. A valuation ratio being below its own average does not by
itself reverse an ownership trend. The signal that India is being re-bought is the **weight**
turning up, not the P/E going lower.
