# Every India "diversification" win has rotated exposure onto one counterparty: Washington

Produced 2026-08-27 by a 21-agent workflow — 8 collectors, 8 adversarial verifiers, four
challenge archetypes, one synthesis. **18 verifier flags raised. This is the deepest finding of
the session and it was invisible to every single-dimension pass.**

## The finding

**Every one of India's diversification wins over five years has rotated exposure away from Russia
and the Gulf and onto the courts, legislature and executive of the United States.**

| "Diversification" | What it actually did |
|---|---|
| Remittances rotating to US/UK | **The US is now the largest single source at 27.7%**, overtaking the UAE |
| LPG sourcing | dominant supplier **flipped from the Gulf to the US** |
| Arms imports to France | **but Tejas is gated by a GE F404/F414 engine** |
| Uranium to Canada | US-adjacent nuclear framework |
| The tariff regime | post-IEEPA basis **unconfirmed**, relitigable |
| The rare-earth truce | **a US-China bilateral India was never party to but is bound by** |

**"What five different desks each file as a separate, manageable risk is one exposure wearing five
names."**

**And it clusters in one window:** US midterms **3 November**, the rare-earth truce expiring
**10 November**, H-1B rule finalisation in the same quarter, and a tariff basis that can be
challenged at any point.

## The specific correlation the market is not pricing

**The IT-services surplus and the remittance flow are not independent legs.** The market treats a
**$207bn services surplus** and record remittances as the stable ballast offsetting energy and
manufacturing risk.

**They are two settlement rails on the same variable — US willingness to pay for Indian labour** —
and both face live US legislative risk: the **$103,265 H-1B NPRM** and the **HIRE Act's 25%
outsourcing excise tax, stalled but not dead.**

**Downstream, Bangalore, Hyderabad and Pune take both hits — IT payroll and skilled-migrant
remittances — in the same city, in the same year, invisibly to two different sets of analysts.**

## Where my own framing was too bearish

**FY26's current account deficit was 0.6% of GDP, with a genuine $7.1bn surplus in Q4.** Not a
crisis. **The Falsifier's own self-calibration admitted it oversold the crisis framing.**

**And my Gulf-remittance framing is materially weakened.** RBI's own FY2023-24 survey:

- **GCC share fell from 46.7% (FY17) to 37.9% (FY24)**; UAE alone from 26.9% to 19.2%
- **The US rose from 22.9% to 27.7% — now the single largest source country**
- **UK rose from 6.8% to 10.8%** after the 2021 Migration and Mobility Partnership
- **US + UK + Singapore + Canada + Australia together exceed 50%**

**The diversification away from the Gulf had already happened before the war started.**

**But the honest reading is not reassuring.** The resilience is not counter-cyclical Gulf behaviour
— it is structural rotation that arrived *just before* a Gulf-specific shock. **The exposure did
not disappear; it moved to Washington**, which is precisely the concentration above.

## The verifier caught a figure I published today

**The "$145bn FY26 remittances, no impact from West Asia" claim is UNVERIFIABLE.** Single outlet
(New Indian Express, 20 July 2026). **No PIB or RBI corroboration across five differently-worded
queries.** And the timing is implausible: **FY25's number was not published by PIB until ~10 months
after FY25 closed; this claims FY26's full-year figure was final and quoted only ~4 months after.**

**I put that figure in a knowledge note two hours ago.** It sits directly against **Kerala's own
April 2026 projection of a 20% remittance drop.**

## Other hard numbers worth keeping

- **India imports 88% of its crude, roughly half through Hormuz — against a strategic reserve
  disclosed under RTI as 9.5 days.** Other methodologies give 60 or 74 days (caverns alone versus
  mandated refiner stock). **Unreconciled, and the gap matters enormously.**
- **The rare-earth chokepoint has already fired: Maruti's e-Vitara output cut 69%** in the April
  2025 episode, with Bajaj facing an EV halt and TVS and Ather cutting output.
- **Aluminium export volumes to the EU are already down ~41%** on CBAM, with **no carve-out in
  either 2026 trade deal** — and the UK deal required a steel-safeguard concession to close.
- **Arms diversification is real and monotonic: SIPRI shows Russia's share at 72% → 55% → 36%**
  across three five-year windows. **CAATSA has never been imposed on India**, unlike Turkey.

## The positioning this implies

**Not less India — different India, with explicit hedges.** A 0.6% CAD does not support a broad
de-risk. **The mispricing is that a flat Nifty shows no spread between US-correlated and
domestically-insulated names.**

- **Trim into a dated catalyst:** CBAM-exposed heavy industry (Tata Steel, JSW, Hindalco, Vedanta,
  Jindal Stainless) and the EV/auto-component chain into **10 November** specifically (Maruti,
  Bajaj, TVS, Sona BLW, Motherson, Bharat Forge, Uno Minda, Suzlon).
- **Stop treating large-cap IT as diversification.** It is correlated ballast on the same US
  variable as the remittance story.
- **Prefer revenue that does not route through Washington** — domestic deposit-and-loan banks,
  FMCG, domestic-facing infrastructure, and sourcing beneficiaries (Petronet, GAIL) over the
  refiners absorbing the price shock.
- **Do not rely on the RBI band as an implicit hedge.** Take an explicit USDINR position.

## What would falsify it

Q2/Q3 FY27 CAD prints below 1% of GDP despite elevated oil; the **10 November truce renewing
cleanly** with no repeat of the 2025 production halts; the HIRE Act staying dead and the H-1B NPRM
being blocked or scoped small; RBI's actual reserve series showing reserves flat-to-rising; or FII
flows reversing with **no** differentiation between US-correlated and insulated names — which would
mean the market never priced this as a distinct factor at all.
