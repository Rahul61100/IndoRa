# The India tariff: which instrument is actually in force, with citations

Four days of quoting "18%" and then "~10% under Section 301" without a document. Now resolved to
Federal Register numbers, and three things change materially.

## What is in force today

| | instrument | citation | date |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In force now** | USTR Notice, **Section 301(b) / 304(a)** of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. 2411/2414) | **91 FR 47318**, doc# 2026-15181 | published 2026-07-28, effective ~2026-07-24 |
| Implementing memo | Presidential Memorandum, cites Section 301 only | **91 FR 47717**, doc# 2026-15274 | signed 2026-07-23 |
| The bridge (expired) | Proclamation 11012, **Section 122** balance-of-payments surcharge | **91 FR 9339**, doc# 2026-03824 | signed **2026-02-20**, expired ~2026-07-24 on its 150-day statutory cap |
| Pharma (separate) | Proclamation 11020, **Section 232**, Trade Expansion Act 1962 | **91 FR 18183**, doc# 2026-06956 | signed 2026-04-02 |

Not IEEPA. The Section 122 proclamation was signed **the same day** the Supreme Court struck IEEPA
down, ran its full 150-day statutory life as a bridge, and handed off to Section 301 on expiry.
That is a deliberately constructed legal chain, not improvisation.

## Three things this changes

### 1. The applied rate is 10%, but ~45% of India's exports to the US are exempt

Roughly **$87bn of India's US-bound exports are excluded** via Annex I / Annex II Part A (raw
materials, non-substitutable goods, economy-disruption-risk items). The **effective** rate across
the export basket is therefore materially below 10%, and any sector analysis that applies 10%
across the board overstates the damage — possibly by close to half. **Which lines are exempt is
the question that matters and it is not yet mapped.**

### 2. This is not a US–India dispute at all

USTR initiated **60 simultaneous Section 301 investigations** on 2026-03-12 under the caption
*"Failure to Impose and Effectively Enforce a Prohibition on the Importation of Goods Produced with
Forced Labor."* India sits in the **10% tier alongside Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia
and Pakistan.**

This overturns how the whole trade thread has been framed here. It has been read as bilateral
pressure on India — leverage over Russian oil, a negotiating posture. It is a **global
labour-standards action that India was swept into with 59 other economies.** The political-economy
implications are different in kind: bilateral concessions do not resolve a 60-country programme,
and India's remedy runs through labour-enforcement compliance, not through a deal.

### 3. Generic pharma is a 2028 problem, not a 2026 one

The published schedule: **0% from 2026-08-01 → 100% in August 2028 → 200% in August 2029.**

Two implications pointing opposite ways. The **near-term risk is zero** — a two-year runway that
Indian pharma exposure has been discounted for prematurely. But the **structural risk is worse than
the general tariff**, because Section 232 rests on a formal Commerce/BIS national-security
investigation (its record opened 2025-04-16, predating this entire tariff cycle) rather than on an
emergency declaration. **The Supreme Court's IEEPA ruling has no bearing on it**, and no challenge
to it was found. A BIS notice (doc# 2026-09489, 2026-05-13) creates a company-specific "onshoring
agreement" route to relief — which is the tell that the intent is relocation of manufacturing, not
revenue.

## The 18% is dead, and its origin is now known

It came from a bilateral US–India deal announced 2026-02-02 to 2026-02-11 ("US to cut tariffs on
India to 18%, India agrees to end Russian oil purchases"), administered under **the same IEEPA
reciprocal framework struck down on 2026-02-20** — nine days later. It was never the instrument in
force and is not collected anywhere today.

## Live legal risk, both directions

- **25 Democratic-led states sued on 2026-08-03/04** over the Section 301 forced-labor tariffs.
  Venue reported as the Court of International Trade but **not confirmed against a primary docket**.
  A PIIE piece dated 2026-08-12 is headlined *"Why Trump will lose (again) on the new challenge to
  his tariffs"* — headline confirmed, body not retrievable, so treat as a signal that credible
  trade economists think the position is weak, **not** as an argument.
- **Senate Russia sanctions bill** (Graham/Blumenthal) passed 2026-08-07/08, authorising up to
  **100% tariffs** on countries buying Russian energy, India named. Vote reported as **86-11** by
  most outlets and 86-12 by one; congress.gov returned 403 so the official tally, bill number and
  short title are **unconfirmed**. **House status is genuinely unknown** — targeted searches for a
  whip count or scheduling statement returned nothing. This is a congressionally-enacted tariff
  power, legally distinct from the emergency theory the Court rejected, which is what makes it the
  serious tail risk.
- **Tier-1 transshipment designation** (mid-August 2026, India among 40+ economies, administration
  claiming $19–26bn/yr and ~450,000 jobs — an unverified administration claim): **no Federal
  Register proclamation, notice or duty-rate change was found tied to it.** The designation is a
  label without an instrument. Watch for the instrument.

## Refunds: the numbers in circulation do not reconcile

$100bn reported disbursed by early August (CBP filing, via multiple outlets, **primary not
retrieved**), against pool estimates of $163bn and $166bn that disagree with each other. India's
own share is reported as **>$1bn / $7bn / $10–12bn / $12bn** across four outlets in three months
with no Commerce Ministry or DGFT confirmation. "Total owed" and "total paid" are being conflated
across the coverage. **Do not quote a refund number.**
