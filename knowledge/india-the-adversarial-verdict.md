# India: what four adversarial archetypes actually settled — and what they did not

Synthesis, 2026-08-27. Four agents on one question: a **Falsifier** told to destroy the case, a
**Steelman** told to show me what I was missing, a **First-Principles Reasoner** forbidden from
searching, and a **Base-Rate Finder**. This is the first real conclusion in this workspace.

## Settled: the argument I built two days on is dead

**The ERP frame is finished**, and three independent agents killed it without coordinating:
- The metric has an **R² of 0.5%** against 56.3% for plain P/E; adding the bond term makes
  forecasts *worse*; it held in **2 of 20 countries** ([[the-fed-model-has-no-predictive-power]])
- It was **pre-tax** — after tax it is roughly zero ([[india-erp-after-tax-is-roughly-zero]])
- It used the **wrong duration** ([[first-principles-caught-four-of-my-errors]])
- **The Falsifier itself conceded** its ERP section was "directionally right but not empirically
  pinned down"
- And the **Steelman** added the reason it fails hardest here: it compares a **nominal fixed coupon**
  to a **growing** earnings stream in an economy compounding **nominal GDP at 10-10.5%** — about 50%
  faster than the bond pays. **A negative naive ERP is what you would expect in a high-nominal-growth
  economy, not evidence of overvaluation.**

## Settled: what the bear case actually reduces to

**One argument survived every challenge: nine consecutive years of missing start-of-year consensus.**
FY25 delivered 3.4% against ~15%; FY26 delivered 4.5% against 12-15%.

**The Steelman explicitly conceded this "stands completely unrebutted"** and could not find data
explaining *why* the misses persist — whether analysts are systematically too bullish or something
structural is wrong. **Neither could anyone else.** It is the most important unexplained fact about
Indian equities and I still cannot account for it.

## Settled: what the bull case actually reduces to

**65% of Indian household wealth is still held physically — gold and real estate — not in financial
assets.**

That is a multi-decade financialisation runway that **requires no foreign flows, no re-rating and
no earnings beat** to keep compounding. Mutual fund AUM went ₹80.8 lakh crore (Nov 2025) to
₹85.76 lakh crore (Aug 2026) against that backdrop.

**I never mentioned it once in two days.** It is the strongest structural argument for Indian
equities and the bear case has no answer to it.

## The direct conflict I cannot resolve — and it is the crux

**The two agents flatly contradict each other on private capex, which is the strongest surviving
bear argument.**

| | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Falsifier** | Private capex at a **decadal low of ~33%** of total investment; unlisted firms, who do **85% of investment spending, remain weak** despite record profits | ET Infra Mar-2025; Moneycontrol May-2026 |
| **Steelman** | Private capex **+67% year on year to ₹7.7 lakh crore**; L&T's CFO says the "revival is visible on the ground" | CII via BW Businessworld May-2026; Moneycontrol Jun-2026 |

Both cite 2026 sources. They may be reconcilable — a share-of-total measure against an absolute
growth measure, listed against unlisted — **but I do not know, and this is the single most
load-bearing disputed fact in the entire India question.**

"Record profits with no reinvestment" and "capex up 67%" cannot both describe the same corporate
sector. **Resolving this is the highest-priority research task outstanding.**

## Corrections to my own record, from the bull side

**"Corporate profit-to-GDP at an all-time high" is imprecise.** Motilal Oswal's own commentary on
the same series a year earlier called it a **"17-year high"** — implying FY08 was higher. The
defensible claim is **"highest since the pre-GFC peak."**

**And the Steelman flagged, against its own interest, that FY08's peak also looked structural at
the time and then mean-reverted hard through the 2010s.** That is a bull agent handing the bear
case its best counter-example. It is why the archetype is worth running.

**The margin re-rating does have one genuinely permanent component I had ignored: the September
2019 corporate tax cut, 30%→22% for existing companies and 25%→15% for new manufacturers.** That is
a statutory change to the margin base, not a cycle.

**And PLI is only ~15-18% disbursed** — ₹35,354 crore against a ₹1.97 lakh crore sanctioned outlay
that has triggered ₹2.40 lakh crore of investment. Consistent with
[[announced-is-not-disbursed]], but it cuts both ways: **most of the capex tailwind is still ahead,
not behind.**

## The challenge to my EM framing that I have to take seriously

I read "India trails MSCI EM by 42 points" as an India problem. **The Steelman argues it is
substantially a denominator effect** — EM's +37% is disproportionately a Taiwan/Korea/China
**semiconductor and AI-capex rally**, and India's weight fell because others' rose, not because
India shrank on fundamentals. Four independent outlets framed India's exit from the EM top 10 that
way.

**Both readings are true and they are not exclusive.** India *is* losing the flow contest
([[india-competition-is-north-asia-not-china]]) — but comparing India to a benchmark that has
become concentrated in one global capex cycle is not the same as saying Indian earnings failed.
**I was using one number to make both claims.**

## What no one could establish

- **No historical precedent, with dates and numbers, for what follows this scale of country
  underperformance.** The Steelman called its own contrarian argument "an assertion, not evidence."
- **No base rate for foreign-ownership troughs** — the episode has not ended.
- **Whether profit-to-GDP mean-reverts** — Grantham and Goldman flatly disagree.
- **Why the earnings misses persist.**

## The verdict

**The question was never "are Indian equities cheap." It is "does the multiple hold."**

At the realised ~4% earnings growth, equities clear the after-tax hurdle **if the multiple holds**,
comfortably **if it re-rates toward its 12.6%-below-average norm**, and fail if it de-rates to 18x
or below ([[india-erp-after-tax-is-roughly-zero]]).

**And I have no edge whatsoever on predicting the multiple.** Nobody does.

**So the honest position is neither the bearish one I was building nor the bullish one:**

1. **Indian equities as *a* core holding: yes.** The financialisation runway is real, uncontested
   and doesn't need anything to go right. **The Falsifier itself would not bet against it over
   15-20 years** — only against it as the *automatic default*.
2. **Indian equities as *the* automatic default with no alternative: no.** Nine years of earnings
   misses is unexplained, the capex picture is genuinely disputed, and gold beat equities on every
   horizon.
3. **The short-horizon bucket is where the real damage was.** It needed a 10-12% after-tax edge
   ([[short-horizon-needs-a-10-12-percent-tax-edge]]) and I was running it on a 10-12% *worse*
   analytical frame.
4. **Unfreeze new India positions — but only at the medium and long horizon, and only on
   stock-specific evidence.** With 58% net upgrade breadth and index estimates cut 9%, the
   opportunity is in selection, which is exactly what the FII futures book expresses.
