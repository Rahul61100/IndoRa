# India's capex cycle is Delhi's, not private industry's — and it is front-loaded

Verified 2026-08-26. **This materially qualifies the L&T and capital-goods theses**, which I had
framed as a private capex cycle.

**Central government and CPSEs are doing the spending:**
- FY27 budget capex target **₹12.12 lakh crore**, fiscal deficit pegged at 4.3% of GDP
- **Q1 FY27 central capex grew 23-24% year on year, with June alone up 66%**
- **CPSE capex +26% YoY in the June quarter, and CPSEs had already spent 25% of their full-year
  target by end-Q1** — ahead of straight-line pace
- Q1 fiscal deficit ₹3.08-3.1 lakh crore, **18.2% of the full-year target**, explicitly attributed
  by coverage to the capex push rather than revenue weakness

**States are not:**
- **April 2026 state capex utilisation: 1.85% of budgeted.** Wide dispersion, Odisha leading and
  Punjab lagging.
- On 1 August the Centre released **₹1,09,019 crore** of tax devolution explicitly framed to boost
  state capex — itself the signal that the Centre saw states behind pace
- States' aggregate fiscal deficit is projected to *moderate* to 3.4% of GSDP, so the fiscal room
  exists and simply is not being deployed

**And the market is already asking whether it holds.** On **14 August a Finance Ministry official
publicly denied any instruction to slow or defer capex** — a denial only issued because the
question was being asked.

**Why this matters to the book:** front-loading is not the same as strength. Capex that is 25% of
target at the end of Q1 and 66% year-on-year in a single month is a run-rate that mathematically
decelerates in H2 unless the budget is raised. And a government-funded cycle is a **fiscal
decision**, revisable at the next budget, not a corporate investment cycle with its own momentum.

It also reconciles a puzzle. Equipment and automation order intake is genuinely strong (Siemens
+32.6%, ABB +25%) while aggregate capital-goods order inflow excluding L&T was **down** year on
year and infrastructure EPC inflow barely moved
([[india-capex-divergence-is-execution-not-demand]]). The answer is that this is a narrow,
publicly funded, electrification-weighted cycle — not the broad private capex revival the sector
narrative implies.

**How to apply:** track **monthly central capex against budget run-rate and CPSE spend** as the
actual driver — neither is currently collected. Treat any H2 deceleration as the thesis test. And
stop describing this as a private capex cycle.
