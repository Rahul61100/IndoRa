# The US had two shutdowns in 2026 — one 76 days, the longest ever — and tariff refunds blew a hole in the deficit

Verified 2026-08-26. **Confidence: DEGRADED in parts** — several government sites (CBO.gov,
SCOTUS.gov, Defense.gov, OCC.gov) blocked automated access throughout, so some figures rest on
secondary reporting. Flagged inline.

**Two facts I did not have, and they reframe the US macro data I quoted this morning:**

## 1. Two government shutdowns in 2026

- **31 January – 3 February 2026**
- **14 February – 30 April 2026 — 76 days, the longest shutdown in US history**

I read July payrolls at −23,000 and May-June revisions at −103,000 as clean evidence of a
softening labour market ([[ai-capex-depreciation-is-the-1999-tell]]). **A 76-day shutdown ending
30 April sits directly inside that window.** It does not erase the softening, but it contaminates
the read, and I did not know to adjust for it.

**Next funding deadline: 4 December 2026** — pushed from 30 September by stopgap. Note the timing:
**one month *after* the midterms**, so the FY2027 appropriations fight lands in a lame-duck
Congress that already knows the incoming makeup. Two shutdowns already this year makes that a live
risk, not a formality.

**No debt-ceiling deadline before 2027** — OBBBA raised it $5tn to $41.1tn.

## 2. The tariff refunds are a real fiscal hole

The Supreme Court voided IEEPA tariff authority on 20 February
([[CORRECTION-ieepa-tariffs-were-struck-down]]). The bill is now arriving:

- **~$166bn was collected historically under IEEPA — the refund universe**
- **~$100bn certified or refunded as of a CBP court filing dated 5 August 2026**, across 330,000+
  importers and 53 million+ entries
- Monthly pace: **May $22bn, June $49.1bn, July $33.4bn**

**Fiscal consequence:** CBO's February baseline had FY2026 at $1.9tn. **Actual run-rate through ten
months is $1.799tn and the full year is tracking ~$2.1tn — roughly $200bn above the original
estimate**, with CBO revising its ten-year deficit up $2.0tn on 5 March largely for this reason.

**July 2026 alone: a $432bn monthly deficit — a record — with interest on the debt at $117.5bn,
up 28% year on year.** Total public debt **$40.05tn**. The 30-year subsequently pushed **past
5.3%**.

**Moody's downgraded the US from Aaa to Aa1 on 16 May 2025** (not 2026, as I had it loosely),
citing successive administrations' failure to reverse deficits and interest costs, projecting debt
at ~134% of GDP by 2035. **No 2026 follow-up statement from Moody's was found** — that is a gap,
not reassurance.

## 3. Section 232 survived, and Canada is escalating

The IEEPA ruling did **not** touch Section 232, so steel and aluminium protection is intact —
Nucor, Steel Dynamics and Cleveland-Cliffs are **not** losers from the ruling. New Section 232
actions through 2026: pharmaceuticals, aluminium/steel/copper, aircraft and engines, polysilicon,
unmanned aircraft.

**Canada sequence:** 21 July, 50% tariffs on autos, alcohol and dairy · 18 August, a three-day
suspension during talks · **22 August, talks collapse and the 50% tariffs take effect on ~$20bn of
goods**, with PM Carney vowing "dollar for dollar" and calling it commercially "at war" ·
**24 August, further escalation announced — Canadian autos, trucks, parts and steel to 50%
effective 1 January 2027.**

**How to apply:** adjust every US labour and activity series in H1 2026 for the 76-day shutdown
before drawing a trend. Put **4 December** in the calendar. And treat the deficit overshoot as
partly a **one-off refund event**, not purely structural deterioration — which cuts both ways for
the term-premium thesis ([[us-fiscal-downgrade-and-term-premium]]).
