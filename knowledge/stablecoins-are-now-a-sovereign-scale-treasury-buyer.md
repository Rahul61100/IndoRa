# Stablecoin issuers are now a top-20 foreign holder of US Treasuries

Verified 2026-08-26. The most important structural fact in crypto, and it is a *rates* fact.

- **Total stablecoin market cap hit an all-time high of $320bn in May 2026**; supply grew **14.3%
  year on year**, from $269.4bn in August 2025 to **$308.0bn in August 2026**
- **Stablecoin issuers' T-bill holdings collectively rank them a top-20 foreign holder of
  short-term US Treasuries. Tether alone sits around 17th** — between sovereigns.
- Tokenised real-world assets hit an all-time high of **$28.9bn in May 2026**: tokenised
  Treasuries **$16.1bn** (from ~$8.9bn at the start of the year), tokenised stocks **$2.41bn**,
  up 20.4% to a record
- The two largest tokenised-Treasury funds are **Circle USYC and BlackRock BUIDL** at roughly
  $2.4-3.0bn each, with USYC overtaking BUIDL for the top spot in early 2026

**Why it matters, in three directions:**

1. **Crypto is now short-rate-dependent in a way it never was.** Issuer revenue *is* the T-bill
   yield. If the Fed cuts, stablecoin issuer economics compress; if it hikes
   ([[fed-may-hike-next]]) they expand. Circle's equity is a leveraged bet on the front end.
2. **It closes a loop with US fiscal.** Growing stablecoin supply is incremental structural demand
   for T-bills at exactly the moment deficits are pushing the long end to 5.2%.
3. **It explains the GENIUS Act's yield ban** — issuers keep the carry, holders do not, and DeFi
   is carved out ([[crypto-regulation-timeline-2026]]). That asymmetry is the whole business model
   and it is now written into statute.

**How to apply:** track stablecoin supply as a **liquidity and rates series**, alongside ETF
flows, not as an adoption metric. This is the second confirmed cross-market linkage in the
workspace after the SaaS lead-lag — crypto and US rates are mechanically joined at the front end.
