# Reserves hit a record and the forward book did not move

The number this workspace called its single most important dark figure is now settled from RBI's
own tables.

| | date | net forward position |
|---|---|---|
| last previously obtainable | 31 Mar 2026 | −$103,064mn |
| | 31 May 2026 | −$106,700mn |
| **now obtained** | **30 Jun 2026** | **−$103,326mn** |

Maturity breakdown at 30 June (Bulletin Table 4A, USD mn): long 1,213 / short 104,539 / **net
−103,326**, short in every bucket except a small long book at ≤1 month.

**The secondary claim that RBI "trims short forward dollar position to $72.5 billion" is refuted**
against the primary table. It was never true.

## The part that matters

Over the same stretch, **reserves hit an all-time record of $729.33bn** for the week ended
21 August, up $12.42bn week-on-week — roughly **$47bn above the 3 July trough of $674.2bn.**

So: reserves climbed to a record **while the forward book stayed pinned near $103bn short.**

That kills the reassuring reading. If the reserve build had come from unwinding the forward
defence, the headline would mean the defence was ending. It did not. The two moved independently,
which means the stock number still says nothing about remaining capacity — exactly the trap this
base flagged when it only had the March figure.

## What did change

Monthly net dollar flow turned:

- April 2026: net **sold** ~$8.9bn
- May 2026: net **sold** $6.10bn (bought $22.229bn, sold $28.333bn)
- June 2026: net **bought** $561mn (bought $30.891bn, sold $30.330bn)

**June is the first net-buying month in this sequence.** Spot intervention pressure eased even as
the forward book stayed stretched. That is a real easing and it should be said plainly — the
currency-stress reading is weaker than it was on 27 August, though not absent.

## What this does to the earlier note

[[the-rupee-is-held-up-not-holding-up]] stands on its central claim — the rupee is managed, and a
headline reserve stock is the output of a defence rather than evidence none was needed. The
forward book being *flat* rather than *widening* softens the trajectory, and June's net buying
softens it further. The framing survives; the urgency drops.

Still open: no forward-book figure after 30 June 2026, so there are two months of dark again. And
the ~3.5%/yr rupee depreciation used in the base-rate work remains what was realised *during* this
defence, not a clean assumption.
