# The Hormuz war has already hit India physically — Ras Laffan, 12 dead Indians, 93% LNG collapse

Verified 2026-08-27. **Confidence: REPORTED**, multi-outlet. **I have been treating this conflict
as an oil-price variable. It is a materialised event that has already struck India through energy,
labour and human cost.**

## What actually happened

- **18-20 March 2026 — Iran struck Qatar's Ras Laffan LNG facility**, destroying an estimated
  **17% of Qatar's LNG capacity for three to five years**, a reported **$20bn annual revenue loss**
- **22 June 2026 — a further explosion at the same complex killed 13 people, 12 of them Indian
  workers.** QatarEnergy called it a "technical incident"
- **Qatar has since cut its government budget by 30%**
- **India's Qatar LNG imports crashed 93%**
- **Strait of Hormuz shipping traffic is down 95%** (27 August 2026)
- The war is roughly **six months old**

## Why my framing was wrong

I wrote that Hormuz was "a live war, not a risk premium" and thought that was the correction
([[hormuz-is-a-live-war-not-a-risk-premium]]). **It was still too abstract.**

**This is not a shipping-lane risk. The production facility itself was destroyed.** A tanker route
can be re-routed or escorted; **17% of a country's LNG capacity gone for three-to-five years
cannot.** India's exposure was never the transit — it was a **single point of failure in
production**.

**And it has already killed Indian citizens.** Twelve of them, in one incident, in June. I have
written thirty-odd notes on India and this is the first time that appears in any of them.

## Remittances — the number, and the tension in it

- **FY26 remittances: a record $145bn**, with the government explicitly claiming "no impact of West
  Asia conflicts" (20 July 2026). FY25 was $135.46bn.
- **~3.5% of GDP** (Economic Survey 2025-26 coverage — directionally right, not confirmed against
  a primary document)
- **~$50bn was flagged "at risk"** during the March escalation — implying roughly **a third of
  India's remittances originate in the Gulf**
- **9-10 million Indians work in the GCC**

**The tension is unresolved and it is the most important open question here.** A record year with
"no impact" was claimed in July, three weeks after twelve Indians died at Ras Laffan and four
months after $50bn was called at risk. **Either the labour-market disruption has not shown up in
flows yet, or workers are remitting defensively ahead of expected disruption, or the claim is
optimistic.** A weaker rupee also mechanically inflates the rupee value of a flat dollar flow.

**Kerala — India's most remittance-dependent state — was reported feeling the shock by early
April.**

## What the market is actually pricing

**A stable, boring current-account item — on the strength of one government press claim made during
an active war.** That is a bet on the war staying contained, not a fact about remittance
durability.

And the transmission would not show up where equity analysts look. It shows up in **bank deposit
growth, real estate and gold demand in Kerala, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and Tamil Nadu** — states that
do not appear in Nifty sector weights.

## The correlation I flagged as a hypothesis is now half-realised

I reasoned that **AI disrupting IT services and a Gulf shock hitting remittances would remove both
offsets to the goods deficit at once**, and that the two are usually modelled as unrelated
([[india-external-the-three-body-problem]]).

**The Gulf leg is no longer hypothetical.** It has hit LNG supply, killed Indian workers, and put a
third of the remittance base in a war zone whose host government just cut its budget 30%.

## What to add to the daily pull

Monthly remittance data, Kerala and Gulf-corridor bank deposit growth, LNG import volumes by
source, and the LPG subsidy line — **India reportedly sources ~90% of LPG through or near Hormuz**,
and an LPG shock is a fiscal event, not just a price one.
