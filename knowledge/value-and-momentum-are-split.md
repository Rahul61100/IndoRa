# In India right now, nearly every cheap large cap is in a downtrend

Verified from the 2026-08-26 snapshot. The names screening cheapest on trailing multiples are
uniformly the ones with the worst price structure:

| Name | 1y | vs 200 DMA | RSI | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HDFC Bank | -26.0% | -14.3% | 38 | downtrend, 1.0% off 52w low |
| Infosys | -24.7% | -15.2% | 47 | below 200 DMA |
| TCS | -25.7% | -13.5% | 45 | below 200 DMA |
| ITC | -32.1% | -15.5% | 38 | downtrend |
| ONGC | -1.7% | -10.1% | 32 | downtrend, 0.2% off 52w low |
| Reliance | -7.9% | -7.1% | 46 | downtrend |
| NTPC | -0.7% | -6.5% | 38 | downtrend |

The uptrends are in things already re-rated: BHEL +90.1%, Shriram Finance +81.5%, ABB +50.3%,
Divi's +49.7% (RSI 78, at its 52-week high), Hindalco +49.5%.

**Why it matters:** a "buy the cheap large cap" screen currently produces a portfolio of
confirmed downtrends. That is not automatically wrong — it is what buying a de-rating looks like
— but it must be entered as a staggered accumulation with a horizon, never as a short-horizon
bounce trade with a stop, because there is no technical base to place a stop against.

**How to apply:** a value thesis on a name below its 200 DMA with falling relative strength is a
medium or long horizon idea by definition. If a short-horizon trade is wanted, it needs either a
dated hard catalyst or an actual trend. Related: [[india-is-derating-not-missing-earnings]].
