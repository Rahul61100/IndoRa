## HDFC Bank is a stock-specific problem, not a sector one

**HDFC Bank hit ₹710.00 intraday, a new 52-week low, trading ~₹714.25 (−1.78%) on 1.68x normal
volume pace.** Prior five sessions ranged ₹722-734.

**And the sector went the other way.** Kotak Mahindra, DCB and **ICICI Bank (+1.38%)** all gained;
Bajaj Finance led the session. Coverage named HDFC Bank as *the* drag: "Sensex erases early gains
as HDFC Bank drags."

**This weakens the flow explanation I leaned on yesterday.** Yesterday's positioning research found
FIIs cut HDFC Bank 360bps (~₹35,000 crore) in the March quarter and that financials have been
seeing an FII comeback since 22 August ([[who-is-actually-buying-india]]). If the de-rating were
purely sector flow, ICICI would not be up 1.4% on the same day HDFC Bank makes new lows on double
volume.

**No disclosed cause found.** A filings sweep found **no bulk or block deal in HDFC Bank on
26 August**, no promoter pledge change, no insider disclosure, no rating action, and no regulatory
item across the whole book in seven days. Today's NSE CSV publishes after the close.

**That absence is itself informative:** heavy volume with no disclosure points to an undisclosed
institutional seller working an order, or index-related flow — not news.

## Steel rallied on a political statement — the layer working same-day

**INOX India +6%, HCC +7%, NMDC Steel +5%, Tata Steel a top gainer.**

The driver was not earnings. On **24 August the Commerce Minister publicly urged Japanese firms to
buy Indian steel and backed the safeguard duty.** A ministerial statement moved a sector three days
later.

**This is exactly the transmission the political-economy layer exists to catch**
([[political-economy-layer]]), and it paid off within a session of being added to the loop.

## Two hits on the open book

**1. SBI is the largest selling shareholder in the NSE IPO.** The issue is ~₹30,000-31,500 crore,
**offer-for-sale only**, 4-4.5% of the company, with **SBI selling alongside GIC Re and the PSU
insurers**. I hold SBI on a two-to-three-year credit-cycle thesis. A large OFS by a holding
company is a cash inflow to SBI, not a claim on it — but it is also a signal about how the
government values the stake, and it lands in the same window as the ₹4.72 trillion pipeline
([[nse-ipo-is-the-supply-event-that-matters]]).

**Status: SEBI's observation letter was still pending as of 23 August**, held up by the
SBI/SBICAPS share-allocation split. Target remains a mid-September launch and month-end listing.
**No price band set.** NSE cleared its co-location case with SEBI on 31 July for ₹715-1,491 crore.

**2. The Jio IPO — my Bharti catalyst — has not cleared its regulatory gate.** DRHP filed 19 June;
**SEBI sought clarifications on 25 June; no observation letter, no price band and no listing date
as of 27 August.** Banker valuation chatter of $148-170bn is stale, from October-November 2025.

Bharti Airtel closed **below its 50 DMA yesterday** and is −0.79% today. **The event thesis was
explicitly conditional on the listing happening in the Aug-October window. It is slipping, and the
stock is confirming.**

## Three other things worth holding

- **The rupee is managed, not stable.** The RBI has been visibly defending a **95.4-95.7 band**
  through August against oil and dollar pressure. Today's 95.54 sits inside it. Read rupee
  stability as intervention, not equilibrium.
- **The monsoon deficit widened to ~13%** as of 27 August, with "weakest since 2009" commentary.
  Kharif sowing −1.5%, **paddy −4.4%**. But late-August catch-up softened the output-damage
  narrative. Still a live input into Monday's agri-GVA line.
- **FMCG has seen a $5.2bn FII selloff across twelve consecutive months**, and financials remain
  **cumulatively net-short ₹1.06 lakh crore** even after the recent comeback.
