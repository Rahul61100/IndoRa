# India's capex contradiction resolved: real in large caps, absent in the base — and the conversion rate has collapsed

Verified 2026-08-27 by the Verifier archetype. **This resolves the most load-bearing disputed fact
in the India analysis.** Confidence: high on the mechanism, with one primary-source gap flagged.

## Neither analyst was wrong. Three different series were being conflated.

| Series | What it measures | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) GFCF share of GDP** | economy-wide, actual | **Private share at a decadal / 11-year low** (ICRA, corroborated independently by CNBC-TV18) |
| **(b) Large-corporate reported capex** | ~1,200 CMIE Prowess firms — actual but **narrow panel**, listed-leaning | **+67% to ₹7.7 lakh crore** in the year to Sept 2025 |
| **(c) RBI sanctioned-project pipeline** | forward **intentions**, not spend | +21.5%, later an estimated +54% |

**"Decadal low" and "+67%" were never going to move together. They are not the same metric and not
the same population.**

## The fulcrum finding, and it is worse than either analyst said

**The conversion rate of planned investment into actual fixed assets has collapsed from ~40%
before 2011 to just 10-15% since 2016-17** — "stagnated at about 10%" (CSEP, Oct 2025, citing
RBI's own approved-projects survey). CMIE separately puts project *completion* rates at **9% in
2022-23**.

**And the pipeline itself is tiny: sanctioned investment is 0.7-1% of GDP against 3-6% in the
mid-2000s.**

**So RBI's headline "+21.5%" and "+54%" intentions growth are statistically dramatic moves on an
economically small, poorly-converting base.** A number I would have quoted as bullish is close to
meaningless without the conversion rate beside it.

## Where the record profits are actually going

Not into plant. **Buybacks rose from 16 a year in 2015-16 to roughly 50 a year recently, and the
dividend payout ratio doubled between 2018 and 2024.**

**This connects to a tax change I logged separately and did not link:** Budget 2026 restored
capital-gains treatment on buybacks for non-promoters ([[short-horizon-needs-a-10-12-percent-tax-edge]]),
making them tax-neutral against a market sale again after the punitive Oct-2024 regime. **The
capital-return channel just got cheaper at exactly the moment the capex channel is not being
used.** That is a self-reinforcing loop, not a coincidence.

## The knife-edge indicator

**RBI's OBICUS capacity utilisation: 75.6% in Q3 FY26, up from 74.3%.**

That sits almost exactly on the **~75% threshold** conventionally treated as the trigger for fresh
capacity-building. Firms do not build below it. **Consistent with selective, sector-by-sector
investment — not an economy-wide green light.** One more percentage point either way changes the
read, which makes it worth tracking monthly.

## The national-accounts picture

Aggregate investment share of GDP is **~31%, roughly 5pp below the 2007-08 peak of 36%**.
Manufacturing capital formation is **~3% of GDP, down ~1.5pp since 2011-12**. Machinery and
equipment investment by private non-financial corporates is **down 17 percentage points since
2011-12**.

**Primary-source gap, flagged honestly:** MOSPI's institutional-sector GFCF table could not be
retrieved — 404s on every URL pattern, and its search will not render. Direction and magnitude are
corroborated twice over (CSEP citing national accounts, plus CNBC-TV18's independent "11-year low"),
but **pull the NAS handbook directly before treating the percentage-point figures as load-bearing.**

## The resolution, and what it means for positioning

**"India private capex is turning" is TRUE for large caps with disclosed financials and rising
utilisation. It is FALSE for the unlisted and broader-economy thesis.**

- **The macro read belongs to the bear.** Record profits are demonstrably not being reinvested at
  the base of the corporate pyramid, where **85% of investment spending** happens. A broad,
  index-level or small-and-mid-cap-weighted capex thematic is **premature.**
- **The stock-level read belongs to the bull.** A real, executed, bank-credit-financed acceleration
  is underway in a specific cohort of large, already-capital-intensive corporates — manufacturing
  and services roughly 50/40 — with credit growth to that segment at ~14% in H2 FY26 against ~10%
  in H1.

**This directly settles the L&T question.** I benched the thesis because its headline wins were
European rather than Indian NIP ([[session-2026-08-27-india]]). The Verifier's finding is that
**L&T-type names are the correct investable expression of this cycle** — large, capital-intensive,
above the utilisation threshold — **and a broad capex basket is not.** The vehicle was right; my
reason for doubting it was about *which contracts*, which is a separate question from *whether the
cycle exists*.

**And a caution on the most recent datapoint:** ET on 20 August 2026 — one week ago — reports
"private capex confidence still catching up." **A year after the ₹7.7 lakh crore print, sentiment
has not followed.** Even the participants are not calling it durable.

## The general lesson

**When two credible sources contradict each other on the same word, they are usually measuring
different populations.** The resolution was not to average them or pick one — it was to identify
that "capex" meant three different series. **Ask what the denominator and the universe are before
treating any disagreement as a disagreement.**
