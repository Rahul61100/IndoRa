# India policy: the windfall tax is back, the 2026 state elections are already over

Verified 2026-08-26. **Confidence: DEGRADED** — three of four research passes hit the exhausted
search quota and fell back to news aggregators; RBI, PIB, PRS, CAG and Finance Commission portals
mostly blocked direct access. **The West Bengal and Tamil Nadu results, the current Lok Sabha and
Rajya Sabha seat counts, and the FY28 budget outlook specifically need an ECI/PRS check before
being repeated as settled fact.**

## Two premises I supplied were wrong

**1. The oil windfall tax was REINTRODUCED in March 2026** after a crude spike. It is widely
assumed scrapped. It is not.

**This matters directly: I listed "the return of a windfall levy" as an invalidation condition for
the ONGC thesis. That condition had already triggered five months before I wrote it.** ONGC was
closed for other reasons ([[macro-regime-aug-2026]]), so no money was involved — but the thesis
was written against a fact that was already false, which is worse than being wrong about a
forecast.

**2. The 2026 state elections have already happened.** West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Assam
all voted, with **results on 4 May 2026.** I had been carrying them as forward catalysts.

**Method lesson:** I briefed an agent with assumptions stated as context rather than as questions,
and two of them were false. **Put the premise in the question, not in the framing** — an agent that
inherits a wrong assumption will often research around it rather than challenge it. This one
challenged it, which is the only reason the error surfaced.

## The findings that are best-defensible

**Real-money gaming is effectively finished as a listed theme.** The Supreme Court **entrenched
the real-money-gaming ban in May 2026**, carrying **retrospective GST exposure above ₹1 lakh
crore**. That is a sector-scale write-off from a judicial ruling — the clearest example available
of why [[political-economy-layer]] belongs in the daily loop. In India, a court can end a business
model faster than any earnings cycle.

**A securities transaction tax hike landed in February 2026**, and the market reacted sharply. I
had not accounted for it at all. It compounds the SEBI F&O curbs on the exchange and broker
complex ([[nse-ipo-is-the-supply-event-that-matters]]) — and NSE is being floated on that earnings
base.

**SEBI's own FY26 F&O study found individual trader losses down 18% and the number of active
traders down 20%.** The curbs achieved their stated objective. That is simultaneously good policy
and a permanent reduction in the revenue pool for exchanges and brokers.

**The 16th Finance Commission held devolution at 41%** — no change to the centre-state split, which
removes one source of state fiscal uncertainty.

**The Economic Survey explicitly warned on election-year state cash transfers.** That is the
government's own document flagging the fiscal cost of the transfer schemes, and it bears directly
on the state-capex shortfall already recorded
([[india-capex-is-central-government-and-front-loaded]]) — states spending on transfers are not
spending on capex.

## How to apply

**Judicial and tax risk in India is not tail risk, it is regular risk.** A Supreme Court ruling
with retrospective GST, an STT hike, a reintroduced windfall levy and F&O curbs all landed inside
eighteen months, each materially repricing a sector. None appeared in any price series or set of
financial statements before it happened, and all were publicly trackable.

Add to the daily political-economy pull: **pending Supreme Court and high court matters affecting
listed companies, and any tax or levy change with retrospective effect.**
