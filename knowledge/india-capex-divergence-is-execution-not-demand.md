# India's capital-goods vs infrastructure gap is an execution story, not a demand story

Verified 2026-08-26 — answers the open question from the 2026-08-26 India note.

The puzzle: our constituent baskets show **capital goods +30.2% over a year with 100% of members
above their 200 DMA**, while Infra-Realty-Cement is **+0.3% over a year**, and the Nifty
Infrastructure index is roughly flat.

The resolution is that the two are not measuring the same thing.

- **Order intake at the equipment and automation names is strong**: Siemens +32.6%, ABB +25%.
  Demand is broad — infrastructure, residential real estate, quick commerce, mining, food and
  beverage.
- **But aggregate capital-goods order inflow excluding L&T was ₹32,400 crore in Q3 FY26, down
  year on year.** The strength is concentrated in electrification and industrial automation, not
  spread across the sector.
- **Infrastructure EPC order inflow barely moved** — ₹565bn in Q4 FY26 against ₹548bn in Q3.
- And within EPC the dispersion is enormous: **Ceigall and KNR performed strongly while PNC
  Infratech and HG Infra missed their full-year targets.** PL Capital's framing is that order
  inflow improved but **EPS momentum stayed soft**.

**Why it matters:** the capex cycle is real, but its economics accrue to whoever has pricing power
and clean execution — equipment makers with proprietary product — and not to whoever wins the
tonnage. EPC is a working-capital business where delays destroy the margin the order implied.

**How to apply:** buy the capex theme through **equipment and electrification**, not through EPC
contractors, unless the specific contractor has a demonstrated execution record. This also
sharpens the standing L&T note in `positions/open-theses.md` — L&T is both, and is the laggard of
its own sector at +12.3% against ABB's +50.3%. Screen EPC on order-to-revenue conversion and
working-capital days, not on order book size.
