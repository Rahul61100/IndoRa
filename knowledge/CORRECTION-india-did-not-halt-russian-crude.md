# CORRECTION: India did not halt Russian crude — it was outbid and disrupted

**This corrects a claim I made on 2026-08-25 and repeated since.** I wrote that India "halted
Russian crude purchases" as a commitment under the February 2026 US trade deal. That is wrong in
mechanism and wrong in degree.

**What actually happened:** Russian barrels to India are falling from a **2.7 mb/d peak in
June-July to roughly 1.8-2.0 mb/d in August 2026**. A reduction, not a halt. And the drivers are
commercial and operational, not a policy commitment:

- **Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian export terminals** cutting available supply
- **Refinery turnarounds** at MRPL and Panipat
- **China outbidding India** for the discounted barrels, pushing India toward costlier West
  African and Americas crude

Russia is now itself importing gasoline *from India* amid domestic shortages — a measure of how
tight Russian refining has become.

**Why the distinction matters enormously:** a policy halt is reversible by policy and is
compensated elsewhere in a negotiation. Being **outbid and supply-disrupted** is neither. India
has lost its discount arbitrage for reasons entirely outside its control, and there is no
diplomatic lever that restores it.

**The cost, quantified:** India's crude import bill is **up 56.5% year on year to $63.4bn for
April-July 2026**, while import volumes were roughly flat (81.9 MMT against 81.5 MMT). **That is
almost entirely price.** Wholesale inflation responds faster than CPI, with second-order pressure
on airlines, logistics and petrochemicals.

**How to apply:** this is the clearest single-country macro transmission in the whole risk map and
it feeds directly into positions already held. Higher landed crude → wider current account →
rupee pressure ([[india-in-usd-is-the-return-that-matters]]) → RBI cannot cut into an inflation
impulse ([[rate-cut-cycle-is-over]]) → higher domestic yields. Every Indian bank position in the
book sits downstream of that chain.

**Method lesson:** I took a trade-deal summary at face value and treated a negotiating headline as
an operational fact. Verify what is actually flowing, not what was announced.
