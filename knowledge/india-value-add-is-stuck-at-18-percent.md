# India's electronics value-add is stuck at 18-20%, and Dixon's moat is the dependency itself

Verified 2026-08-27. **This is the finding that most damages the manufacturing thesis I have been
carrying, and it hits a position I hold.**

## The dependency, quantified

- **India's goods trade deficit with China crossed $100bn in FY25-26, settling near $112bn** — and
  **H1 CY2026 alone is already $67.1bn**, annualising to **over $130bn.** Up **155% in five years.**
- **Industrial inputs are 98.5% of everything India imports from China.** This is not a
  consumer-goods relationship — it is a manufacturing-input relationship, entirely.
- **China plus Hong Kong supply 56% of India's electronics, telecom and electrical imports.**

## The number that breaks the story

**Domestic value addition in Indian electronics manufacturing: 18-20% as of April 2026.**

**It has been flat since 2023.** A February 2024 projection had it reaching 30% "soon." Two and a
half years later it is still 18-20%.

Meanwhile gross electronics output is headlined at **₹11.3 lakh crore.**

**So: large and growing gross output, built on roughly 80% imported content, with domestic value-add
stuck at a fifth.** That is the "subsidised assembly of imported kits" scenario, and the evidence
supports it rather than contradicting it.

**The arithmetic consequence, which nobody frames this way:** if 80% of output value is imported,
**the effective subsidy per unit of domestic value actually created is roughly five times the
headline PLI rate.** ₹1.9 lakh crore is buying assembly-and-export of largely Chinese-content goods
under an Indian label.

## Dixon — and the third independent reason to be careful

I have already capped Dixon twice: the **memory BOM shock**
([[memory-shortage-is-a-tax-on-device-makers]]) and **Mobile PLI 2.0 having disbursed nothing**
([[announced-is-not-disbursed]]).

**This is the third, and it is the most structural: Dixon's competitive moat and its input
dependency are the same fact.**

Press Note 3 does not eliminate Chinese participation — it **rations** it. The Chinese partner is
capped at a minority stake and the Indian partner must hold 51%+. **That creates scarcity value for
whichever Indian firm the government blesses as the approved counterparty** — and **Dixon received
both the Vivo JV (July 2026, Dixon 51%, targeting ~₹30,000 crore revenue) and the HKC display JV
(March 2026).**

**A more open FDI regime would compete that scarcity away.** So part of Dixon's re-rating is a rent
created by the restriction, not by manufacturing capability — **and the same restriction is what
makes the input dependency unavoidable.**

**And the exposure is close to 1:1 on revenue.** If ~80% of a smartphone's value is the imported
part being assembled, an input shock hits revenue almost proportionally, while only ~20% of the
value is the part Dixon actually adds.

**Also relevant: BYD's ~$1bn EV JV with Megha was rejected in 2023.** The policy is
sector-differentiated — consumer electronics assembly gets approved, strategic and dual-use sectors
do not. That is discretionary practice, not a published rule.

## The rare-earth scheme has the same defect

**India's ₹7,280 crore REPM scheme subsidises magnet SINTERING, not oxide SEPARATION.** The
ore-to-oxide midstream step is the actual chokepoint, and it stays China-dependent.

**So even if all 6,000 MTPA of sintering capacity is built, India may simply be importing Chinese
oxide to sinter domestically** — self-reliance that relocates the final tenth of the value chain
rather than securing it.

Twenty bidders including L&T, Coal India, Vedanta, Hindustan Zinc and Nalco submitted by August
2026 after the deadline was extended three times.

## The date India does not control

**The 10 November 2026 rare-earth suspension is a US-China bilateral truce** — agreed Trump-Xi on
30 October 2025, implementation from 9 November 2025, a one-year window tied to a deal that also
cut fentanyl-related tariffs.

**India has no seat at that table and no lever over whether it is renewed.**

I logged this date as a US risk ([[china-rare-earth-deadline-is-november-10]]). **It is an India
risk that India cannot negotiate.** If US-China relations deteriorate over Taiwan or export-control
enforcement — matters entirely unrelated to India — **India absorbs the same collateral supply shock
on the same timeline.**

There is precedent: Bajaj halted Chetak EV production in August 2025 on the magnet shortage; TVS
and Ather were affected. The market treated it as transient.

## The friction is bidirectional now

**China is rejecting up to ~95% of business visa applications from Indian executives** (August
2026), forcing meetings to Singapore and Bangkok. **Foxconn recalled 300+ Chinese engineers from
Indian iPhone plants in July 2025.**

## The honest conclusion

**The 2026-2030 window is structurally a period of RISING absolute China-input dependency alongside
a FALLING headline narrative of dependency** — because the assembly-led growth strategy and the
de-risking strategy run on different clocks, **and the growth clock is faster.**

Solar upstream localisation is mandated only from **June 2028**. Chip fabs realistically produce
**2027-28** and substitute one foreign dependency for another. A genuine value-chain delinking is
**not achievable this decade.**

**Action: Dixon stays capped, and the thesis is now explicitly a bet on the policy rent persisting
rather than on manufacturing capability compounding.** Those are different investments and I had
been buying the second while owning the first.
