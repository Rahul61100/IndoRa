# The bear case on India that survives — and it has nothing to do with the ERP

Verified 2026-08-27 by the Falsifier archetype, which **self-assessed at 60-65% conviction, not
90%** — the honesty that makes it usable. **Confidence: REPORTED**, post-cap sourcing.

## The argument that survives everything

**Record corporate profits alongside private capex at a decadal low.**

- Corporate profit-to-GDP is at an **all-time high of 5.2%**
- **Private capex sits at a decadal low of ~33% of total investment**
- **Unlisted firms supply ~85% of investment spending and remain weak** despite those record profits

**This is a corporate sector that does not believe its own growth story enough to fund it.** Record
margins with no reinvestment is cash being hoarded, not deployed.

**That argument does not depend on the ERP, the multiple, or any valuation metric.** It survives
the demolition of the Fed model ([[the-fed-model-has-no-predictive-power]]) entirely, because it is
an observation about corporate behaviour rather than about price. **It is now the strongest bear
argument I hold.**

## Three facts I did not have

**India was excluded from the MSCI EM top 10 for the first time in 26 years** (June 2026). That
mechanically caps future passive inflow — a structural change to the buyer base, not a sentiment
reading. Adds to [[india-lost-half-its-em-weight]].

**The Sensex has gone 697 days without a record high — its worst two-year stretch since 2012.**

**Foreign equity via LRS carries a real, quantifiable tax penalty**, which I had not costed:
**20% TCS upfront** on remittance (creditable, but a permanent cash-flow drag), STCG at **slab**
rather than 20%, and **LTCG at 12.5% with NO ₹1.25 lakh exemption** — §112A applies only to
STT-paid Indian-listed equity. On a ₹10 lakh gain that is a **~14% higher tax bill from the missing
exemption alone.** The code is structured to keep money onshore.

## The long-grind scenario, with precedent

Not a crash — **prices flat while patience runs out:**
- **Nikkei 1989-2024: ~34 years to reclaim the nominal high.** An entire working life for a round
  trip to zero.
- **US 1966-1982: ~16 years flat nominally**, deeply negative in real terms.
- **India now: the Nifty has been flat for two years.** The Falsifier's point is that this is the
  *opening chapter* of that scenario, not a counterexample to it.

Its quantified case: **5% EPS CAGR** (continuing the current miss rate, not a recession) with the
multiple compressing 20.5x → 14x gives roughly **−7% cumulative nominal total return over five
years**, or about −1.4% a year, against ~5% inflation — **30-35% of purchasing power destroyed**
while a G-sec locks ~6.87%.

**Stated honestly by the agent as a reasoned model, not a sourced forecast.** Plug in 12% growth
and an 18x terminal multiple and the same framework turns positive.

## Structural attacks worth holding

- **Demographics:** ~40% of graduates under 25 are unemployed (~11 million); youth unemployment
  ~16%. A growth story premised on a young workforce that cannot find formal work is a consumption
  ceiling.
- **State capacity:** the government's **own Economic Survey 2025-26** flags "State Government
  Finances Emerge as a Growing Macro Risk." That matches the 1.85% state capex utilisation already
  recorded ([[india-capex-is-central-government-and-front-loaded]]).
- **Governance:** SEBI **debarred ZEEL, Punit Goenka and Subhash Chandra for fraud** in August 2026.
- **Formalisation is contested, not settled** — "GST created a formalisation cascade" against "deep
  unease in the informal sector as the tax axe falls." The bull case treats it as a completed
  ratchet; the evidence shows an uneven, resisted process.

## Where the Falsifier conceded

Asked to drop the adversarial stance, it named its own weak points — which is exactly why the
archetype is worth running:

1. **Its ERP section is "directionally right but not empirically pinned down."** The base-rate work
   shows it is worse than that — the metric has an R² of 0.5%. **The two agents, run independently,
   converge on the ERP argument being the weakest part of the bear case.**
2. **It could not verify SIP behaviour in 2008 or 2020** — the load-bearing gap in its own flow
   attack. It is inferring fragility from two years of mild stress, not a crisis base rate.
3. **Gold's outperformance is significantly a global de-dollarisation trade, not an India-specific
   signal**, and +40% CAGR is a poor multi-decade base case to extrapolate. That directly tempers
   [[gold-has-beaten-indian-equities-on-every-horizon]].
4. It would not bet against Indian equities as **a** core holding over 15-20 years — only against
   them as **the** automatic default. **That distinction is the whole finding.**

## Where this leaves it

The bear case has been stripped of its weakest limb and left with its strongest:
**not "the risk premium is negative", but "the corporate sector is not reinvesting its own record
profits."** One is a discredited ratio. The other is behaviour.
