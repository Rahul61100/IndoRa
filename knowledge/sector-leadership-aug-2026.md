# Sector leadership as of August 2026 — capital goods broadest, FMCG and oil/gas worst

From constituent baskets (median member return, 2026-08-26). Percentage is share of members
above their 200 DMA — the breadth number matters more than the return.

**Leading, with breadth:**
- **Capital goods** +30.2% 1y, **100% of members above 200 DMA** — the broadest uptrend in the
  market. ABB +50.3%, BHEL +90.1%, Siemens +26.4%. L&T at +12.3% is the *laggard* of its own
  sector. Corroborated by order intake: Siemens +32.6%, ABB +25%.
- **PSU banks** +28.9% (SBI), uptrend
- **Defence** +8.7% 1y but +8.0% over a month and +19.7% over six, 75% breadth, median RSI 58.6 —
  re-accelerating
- **EMS/electronics** -12.8% over a year but **+27.9% over three months with 100% breadth** — the
  sharpest turn in the market. Policy-driven: Semicon 2.0 ₹1.27 lakh crore plus Mobile PLI 2.0
  ₹62,500 crore.
- **Autos** +11.9% 1y, +11.9% 3m, 71% breadth. EV sales +83% in July 2026; Maruti crossed two
  lakh domestic units in a month for the first time.

**Lagging, with bad breadth:**
- **FMCG/consumer staples** -20.9% 1y, **25% breadth**, median RSI 38.3 — the worst sector.
  HUL -23.1%, ITC -32.1%, both near 52-week lows.
- **Oil and gas** -1.0% 1y but **0% of members above their 200 DMA** — the worst breadth reading
  in the entire universe. Reliance, ONGC, IOC, BPCL all below.
- **Power/utilities** -5.5% 1y, 20% breadth, median RSI 38.1
- **IT** -11.4% 1y, 43% breadth, but +5.9% over three months

**How to apply:** treat 0% and 25% breadth readings as disqualifying for any short-horizon idea
regardless of how cheap the name screens. Re-derive this table daily; it is the single most
useful output of the loop. Related: [[breadth-is-split-not-trending]].
