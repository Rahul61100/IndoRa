# 2026-08-26 (third session) — flows collector built, and it disagrees with the narrative

## What changed

**Flows are now collected.** This was the single largest gap in the process — the variable that
had explained more of what happened in all three markets than any valuation metric, and it was
not being recorded at all. `scripts/fetch_flows.py` pulls three public, unauthenticated series
into append-only ledgers:

- **India FII/DII** daily cash-segment net, from NSE
- **Stablecoin total supply**, from DefiLlama
- **Total DeFi TVL**, from DefiLlama

First India print — 26 Aug: **FII net +₹503 crore, DII net +₹6,425 crore.** Both buying, DII
thirteen times the size. That ratio *is* the Indian market's structure right now.

**Note a hard operational constraint:** NSE serves only the latest session. The stablecoin and TVL
series backfilled 3,193 and 3,118 rows respectively, but **a skipped day of FII/DII is permanently
lost.** `fetch_flows.py` now runs first in the daily loop, and a failed source prints loudly rather
than returning empty — a silently missing flow series reads as zero flow, which is a lie in the
worst possible direction.

**Caught a units bug before it poisoned the ledger.** DefiLlama serves raw USD; the field was
labelled millions. Reported stablecoin supply as "$309,022,082.6bn". Deleted and re-pulled.

## The collector immediately validated — and then contradicted — the research

**Validated:** our independently pulled series puts the stablecoin peak at **$320.9bn on
2026-05-17** and August 2026 at **$308.8bn**, against reported figures of "$320bn ATH in May" and
"$308.0bn in August". The collector is trustworthy.

**Contradicted:** the reporting frames stablecoins as a growth story. Our series says supply
**peaked in May and has fallen 3.2% over 90 days**, flat over 30. Meanwhile BTC is +28.2% and ETH
+51.3% over that same three months.

That divergence is the finding. Two pools of money moving in opposite directions:
**ETF creations rising** (TradFi buying a wrapper) while **stablecoin supply falls** (capital
actually resident on-chain and available to deploy). **The rally is funded from outside the crypto
system, not inside it.** Corroborated by DeFi TVL at $87.6bn, **-41.5% over a year**, while the
DeFi token basket is +36.5% over three months — price recovering well ahead of the capital that
would justify it. And by the breadth: Majors 100%, L1-Alt 22%, L2 0%.

A rally on imported liquidity reverses at the speed of the flow that created it. Stablecoin supply
turning up is the confirmation that it has become self-sustaining. It has not.

## A US supply-chain fact landed on an Indian position

**The most useful thing the multi-market universe has done so far**, and it is a risk, not an idea.

Gartner: DRAM and SSD prices **+130% by end-2026**, pushing smartphone prices +13% and PC prices
+17%, and driving **smartphone shipments -8.4% and PC shipments -10.4%** — the steepest device
contraction in over a decade. The damage concentrates in **entry and mid-tier Android**, where
memory is the biggest BOM swing and demand is most price-elastic.

That is exactly **Dixon's** segment. A ~3%-margin contract assembler paid per unit cannot absorb a
BOM shock or pass one on, and a shipment contraction hits volume and margin simultaneously. This
risk was not in the thesis when it was opened. Dixon stays core at half position but is **capped —
no adds** until pass-through terms are checked and shipment data is a tracked input.

The general rule, promoted to knowledge: **when a shortage makes an upstream supplier a star, ask
who is paying downstream and whether we own them.** An India-only view could not have seen this.

## Five other things now understood

**India's capex divergence resolved — it is execution, not demand.** Capital-goods order inflow
*excluding L&T* was ₹32,400 crore in Q3 FY26, **down** year on year; the strength is concentrated
in electrification and automation. Infrastructure EPC inflow barely moved (₹565bn vs ₹548bn) with
huge dispersion — Ceigall and KNR strong, PNC Infratech and HG Infra missing targets. Buy the
capex theme through **equipment, not tonnage**.

**India's supply and bid are both structural and both records.** SIP ~₹31,000 crore a month, a
**25-month DII buying streak worth ₹11.4 trillion**, DII ownership of the Nifty 500 at a record
**21%** — against **245 DRHPs and ₹4.72 trillion** of IPO pipeline including Jio, NSE, PhonePe,
SBI Funds, Zepto and OYO. That ratio explains the whole index: the bid is why India has not fallen
despite de-rating, the supply is why it has not risen despite 18% profit growth.

**India's small-cap premium is two sigma wide with deteriorating earnings.** Smallcap 250 at
**38.3x** against MSCI World Small Cap at 19.5x; small-to-large forward P/E at **1.25x against a
0.9x long-run average**; and **40% of small caps missed Q3 FY26 estimates against 25% of large
caps**. No valuation cushion at all.

**Private credit is the real US risk, and IG spreads are hiding it.** US private credit default
rate hit a **record 6.0% in April 2026**; the "true" rate approaches 5% once liability-management
exercises are counted; the **CCC-minus-single-B spread now exceeds 600bp** while headline spreads
sit near historic lows. This is the same variable underneath the AI-capex story — Oracle raising
$40bn against a -$23.7bn free cash flow deficit, into that market, with the 30-year at 5.2%.

**Ethereum's L2s are a dead sector with three survivors.** Dencun cut fees ~90%, triggering a fee
war that pushed nearly every rollup into losses. **Base was the only profitable L2 in 2025 (~$55mn).**
Base plus Arbitrum hold **77% of L2 DeFi TVL**, top three 83%. Linea's deposits -60%, Blast's TVL
-97%, Kinto shut down. Keeping this as the reference case for **infrastructure with no value
accrual** — the technology worked, throughput rose, fees went to zero, and no holder captured
anything. Useful the next time any market's infrastructure thesis claims adoption implies returns.

**And stablecoins are now a rates instrument.** Issuers are collectively a **top-20 foreign holder
of short-term US Treasuries, Tether around 17th.** Issuer revenue *is* the T-bill yield — so a Fed
hike expands their economics and a cut compresses them. Crypto and US front-end rates are now
mechanically joined. Second confirmed cross-market linkage after the SaaS lead-lag.
