# Playbook — the political economy layer

**Added 2026-08-26 after a correct criticism: the analysis was running a Western textbook on a
market where proximity to power is a pricing factor.**

Quantitative and fundamental work answers *what a business earns*. In India particularly — but
increasingly in the US and in crypto too — a large part of the return comes from something the
financial statements do not contain: **who gets the licence, the order, the subsidy, the spectrum,
the approval, the bailout, or the forbearance.** That is a knowable, publicly documented variable
and it was absent from every note written before today.

This runs **every day**, alongside price and flows.

## The four questions

### 1. Who is positioned, and where?
- **Promoter activity** — stake sales, open-market buying, creeping acquisition, and above all
  **pledging** (increases are a warning; releases are a signal). The single best insider tell in
  India.
- **Bulk and block deals** — who is exiting at a discount, and to whom.
- **Domestic fund positioning** — monthly MF portfolio disclosures. What are the large domestic
  managers actually buying, not what are they saying.
- **FII sectoral flows and stock-level ownership** — where is foreign ownership at multi-year
  lows (a contrarian setup) versus still-crowded.
- **Insider transactions** under SEBI PIT disclosure, especially clusters.
- **Disclosed portfolios of the large individual investors** — anything above 1% must be filed.

### 2. Who holds the levers?
Track the people, and track when they change:
- **India** — RBI Governor and deputies, SEBI Chair, Finance Secretary, Chief Economic Adviser.
- **US** — Fed Chair, Treasury, SEC, CFTC, Commerce, USTR, FTC and DOJ antitrust.
- A change of guard frequently changes policy direction before it changes any published rule.

### 3. Who benefits from what the state is doing?
- **Which listed entities actually received** PLI disbursements, defence orders, transmission
  awards, spectrum, mines, ports, airports — **disbursed, not announced.** The gap between the two
  is where the real information is.
- **Disinvestment and government stake** — the divestment pipeline, OFS, special dividends and
  buybacks (a government extracting cash from a PSU is a different signal from a government
  building one).
- **Direct state participation** — government equity stakes, loans, guarantees, golden shares,
  directed procurement.
- **Business-group ascendancy** — which houses are winning and which are fading. In India this is
  a persistent, tradeable regime, not noise.

### 4. What is on the political calendar, and what does it move?
- **Elections** — state and national. Pre-election fiscal behaviour is measurable: capex,
  subsidies, transfers, deficit slippage.
- **Budget** — allocations by sector, and the run-rate against them through the year.
- **Legislation and rulemaking pending** — and who each item helps or hurts.
- **Regulatory and judicial risk** — SEBI orders, RBI business restrictions, CCI actions, court
  rulings. In India these move stocks harder and faster than earnings do.

## Rules for doing this honestly

**This is factual mapping of the public record. It is not conspiracy analysis.**

- Use **public filings, published policy, reported appointments, court and regulatory orders**.
- Where something is contested or under appeal, **say so**.
- **Never speculate about undisclosed relationships, motives or wrongdoing.** The observable fact —
  this group won this contract, this promoter pledged this stake, this regulator issued this order
  — is both sufficient and defensible. Inference beyond it is not research.
- Distinguish **legislated / announced / speculated** every time. Most market damage comes from
  treating an announcement as a disbursement.

## What to add to the daily loop

Insert between the flows step and the macro step in `daily-research-loop.md`:

- Any promoter pledge change, stake sale or bulk deal in the book or on the watchlist
- Any regulatory or judicial action affecting a held name
- Any policy or appointment news that changes who benefits
- Position against the political calendar — what is the next dated event, and what does it move
